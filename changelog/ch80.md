# 3 October 2026 · Section 80.7, the arithmetic of a design

The tenth and last bank to take a predict-the-number section. Chapter 80 already has the ROI
calculation (Q80-016) and the design discussions; this section is the capacity and reliability
arithmetic that makes a design argument concrete rather than a preference.

**Section 80.7, "Predict the number: capacity, availability, and the arithmetic of a design."**
3,795 words, 6 core questions (Q80-030 to 035) and 8 rapid-fire rows (Q80-036 to 043), taking the
chapter from 29 questions to 43. Numbered 80.7; nothing renumbered.

| | |
|---|---|
| Q80-030 | 99.9% is 8.76 hours a year, 43.8 minutes a month |
| Q80-031 | Five services at 99.9% in series give 99.5% — 43.7 hours a year |
| Q80-032 | A million events a day is 11.6/sec average and 55.6/sec if 80% lands in four hours |
| Q80-033 | A 20-call page hits the p99 tail 18.2% of the time |
| Q80-034 | 100 TB, RF 3, 30% growth, 70% utilisation → about 940 TB provisioned, not 300 |
| Q80-035 | Little's Law: a 100-connection pool serves 500 req/s at 200 ms and 50 at 2 s |

Q80-031 and Q80-033 are deliberately paired: the availability and the latency arguments against
decomposing a synchronous path, both as numbers rather than as opinions. Q80-035 explains why an
incident goes from "a bit slow" to "entirely down" without any further trigger, which is the single
most useful thing in the section.

## The questions that carry the sections

**Q80-044, the postmortem.** A nightly pipeline failed silently for a week. The structure has six
rows and the one candidates leave out is **why detection took a week** — because the bug is one bug
and the detection gap is every future bug. The usual honest conclusion is that the pipeline alerted
on crashes and had no freshness or volume check, so a job that completed having written nothing
passed. Blameless is defined as the opposite of "nobody is responsible": the question is which part
of the system permitted it, because a postmortem that lands on "X should have noticed" produces no
change and guarantees the next one is concealed.

**Q80-045, disaster recovery.** Two numbers decide the design — RPO and RTO — and the strong answer
asks for them per system before anything else, then asks which event is being recovered from, since a
region outage, a corrupted table and a deleted account have almost nothing in common. The senior move
offered is differentiating by system: Riverstone's order ledger may warrant an RPO near zero while
the warehouse is rebuildable from source and needs a documented procedure rather than a replica.
The question that finds the real state is "when did we last restore this into a working system?"

**Q80-053, the cutover.** Five stages, and stage 2 — an **automated output comparison on real
traffic** — is named as the one that finds everything, because it reliably discovers that the old
system did something nobody documented. Chapter 14, §14.11's reconciliation discipline applies
directly. The thing everyone forgets is the decommission date, set when the migration starts, because
maintaining two systems is the most expensive steady state available and the default outcome.

**Q80-061, the bottlenecked platform team.** Classified four ways — many similar small requests,
few large bespoke ones, approvals, or the team firefighting itself — because the four have four
different fixes and three of them are not headcount. Conway's Law is the frame: a single central
route to production produces a single architectural chokepoint whatever the headcount. The answer
that earns the role is "I would spend a week classifying the queue."

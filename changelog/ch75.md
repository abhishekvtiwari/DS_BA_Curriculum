## 75.8 is measured on Riverstone's real Q4 data, and two answers contradict the cliché

The chapter's own rule is that every figure is a labelled assumption or a sourced number. This
section uses `companion/ch14/clean_truth_orders_q4_2025.csv` throughout — 25,832 order lines,
14,372 orders, ₹44.26 crore net — which the chapter barely used before.

**Q75-047, diagnosing December.** Revenue is ₹9.11 crore against October's ₹18.85 crore. The
December data stops on the 28th, and scaling to 31 days adds only **₹0.98 crore of a ₹9.74 crore
gap** — so incompleteness explains 10% and no more. Decomposing the rest: orders fell 18% while
revenue per order fell **41%**, and lines per order held at 1.80. Basket size did not change, so the
fall is entirely in value per line, which points at price, mix or discount and away from demand.
The question ends by naming the one query that would discriminate, rather than naming a cause.

**Q75-048 kills 80/20 with a measurement.** The top 20% of customers produce **41.4%** of revenue,
not 80%; the top 1% produce 3.5%. The strategic consequence runs opposite to the cliché: a key-
account programme on the top 42 customers is worth 0.35% of revenue if it lifts them 10%, no single
customer is a material risk, and broad-based initiatives beat targeted ones here.

**Q75-049 reports a negative result as a result.** Simpson's paradox needs the mix to differ between
groups *and* the within-group rates to reverse. Measured: segment mix is near-uniform across
branches (retail about half of every branch) and within-segment revenue per line agrees within about
3%. So the paradox is absent, which licenses the simple branch comparison against the obvious
objection — and the check turned up a better fact as a by-product: wholesale lines are worth about
**40% more** than retail or hospitality lines, consistently, in all four branches.

**Q75-050.** Mumbai takes ₹15.73 crore and Kolkata ₹5.45 crore — a **2.9× spread in total against a
2.5% spread in revenue per order** (₹30,241 to ₹30,994). The branches differ in volume, not in
selling, which turns a performance conversation into a territory one. The answer then names the
three denominators that would settle it (revenue per salesperson, growth, cost to serve) and says
plainly that none of them is in this file.

## Sources checked for definitions and the one benchmark (28 September 2026)

Several reference sites (Investopedia, CFI, Wikipedia, Wall Street Prep) are blocked by this container's proxy; the definitions below were checked through search-result extracts of these pages.
- DIO, DSO, DPO, CCC = DIO + DSO − DPO, and operating cycle = DIO + DSO: Corporate Finance Institute, "Cash Conversion Cycle" (corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/accounting/cash-conversion-cycle/); Wall Street Prep, "Cash Conversion Cycle" (wallstreetprep.com/knowledge/cash-conversion-cycle-ccc/).
- NPS: % promoters (9–10) − % detractors (0–6) on a 0–10 recommend question: Bain & Company, "Measuring Your Net Promoter Score" (netpromotersystem.com/about/measuring-your-net-promoter-score/).
- Debt-to-equity has a broad (total liabilities) and a narrow (interest-bearing debt) version: Business Literacy Institute and Wikipedia, "Debt-to-equity ratio" (search extract). This is why 23.6's rename to "liabilities-to-equity" is safe.
- OEE = availability × performance × quality: oee.com, "OEE Factors".
- LTV:CAC 3:1 is a rule of thumb from subscription software, popularised by David Skok (Matrix Partners, *For Entrepreneurs*): Harvard Business School Online, "LTV/CAC Ratio" (online.hbs.edu/blog/post/ltv-cac); Klipfolio LTV:CAC KPI page. The text now calls it "a widely quoted rule of thumb" from subscription software instead of a "textbook" benchmark, and calls the 5-year cap "this chapter's" choice instead of "standard".

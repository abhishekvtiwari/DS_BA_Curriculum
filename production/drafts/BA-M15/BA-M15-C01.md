It connects each approved topic to risk, control, compliance, resilience, sector context, and integrative professional judgment while keeping decisions, evidence, ownership, and limitations visible.

## Learning outcomes

After completing this chapter, the learner should be able to define every controlled topic, select it for an appropriate business question, identify required evidence and governance, recognize misuse, and explain the boundary of a defensible conclusion.

## BA-15-C01-T01 - risk identification

### Concept

In Risk and Controls, risk identification means systematically discovering uncertain events, conditions, causes, and consequences that may affect objectives. The definition must be tied to a stated purpose, boundary, stakeholder, and accountable decision rather than used as an isolated label.

### Analysis and application

Apply risk identification by documenting the triggering question, inputs, method, assumptions, dependencies, expected output, owner, and decision rule. Compare current evidence with a relevant baseline, target, obligation, or alternative and retain enough provenance for another reviewer to reproduce the reasoning.

### Quality checks and limitations

Apply jurisdiction- and sector-specific expert review, least privilege, proportional controls, documented exceptions, residual-risk acceptance, and independent assurance. For this topic, test normal, boundary, adverse, and exceptional conditions; record uncertainty, conflicts, access limits, and the escalation or response path before relying on the result.

## Integrated risk and controls model

Start with the decision and affected people, then define scope, terminology, evidence, assumptions, authority, and timing. Select the least complicated method capable of answering the question. Reconcile outputs, challenge causal claims, assess displaced risks and benefits, and establish ownership for monitoring, change, exceptions, and retirement.

## Common misconceptions

- A familiar label does not guarantee a shared definition.
- More precision, documentation, tooling, or ceremony does not compensate for weak evidence.
- A successful local outcome may transfer cost or risk elsewhere.
- A baseline, target, model, plan, or control must be maintained when its context changes.
- Analysts structure and evidence decisions but do not replace accountable authorities or qualified specialists.

## Ethics, controls, and professional boundaries

Identify who gains, who carries cost or risk, and whose evidence may be missing. Minimize sensitive data, protect confidentiality, include accessible participation, preserve dissent, and communicate uncertainty near the decision. Escalate legal, financial, safety, security, privacy, regulatory, statistical, clinical, and other specialist questions to qualified owners.

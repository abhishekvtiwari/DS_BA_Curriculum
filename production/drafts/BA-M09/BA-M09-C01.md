## Integrated finance foundations model

Start with the decision and affected people, then define scope, terminology, evidence, assumptions, authority, and timing. Select the least complicated method capable of answering the question. Reconcile outputs, challenge causal claims, assess displaced risks and benefits, and establish ownership for monitoring, change, exceptions, and retirement.

## Common misconceptions

- A familiar label does not guarantee a shared definition.
- More precision, documentation, tooling, or ceremony does not compensate for weak evidence.
- A successful local outcome may transfer cost or risk elsewhere.
- A baseline, target, model, plan, or control must be maintained when its context changes.
- Analysts structure and evidence decisions but do not replace accountable authorities or qualified specialists.

## Ethics, controls, and professional boundaries

Identify who gains, who carries cost or risk, and whose evidence may be missing. Minimize sensitive data, protect confidentiality, include accessible participation, preserve dissent, and communicate uncertainty near the decision. Escalate legal, financial, safety, security, privacy, regulatory, statistical, clinical, and other specialist questions to qualified owners.

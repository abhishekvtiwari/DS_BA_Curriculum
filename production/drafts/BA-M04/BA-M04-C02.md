## Purpose

This chapter develops the controlled theory for process mapping within end-to-end business process analysis. It connects purpose, evidence, notation, measurement, governance, and improvement while preserving the distinction between an observed process, an approved model, and a proposed design.

The chapter contains Phase 1 theory only. It does not contain MCQs, exercises, assignments, implementation labs, guided projects, case studies, capstone work, or release artifacts.

## Learning outcomes

After completing this chapter, the learner should be able to define every controlled concept, select it for an appropriate analytical question, identify required evidence and governance, recognize common failure modes, and explain its relationship to end-to-end process value.

## BA-04-C02-T01 - Flowcharts

### Concept

A flowchart represents the sequence of activities and decisions using a small visual vocabulary. It is useful for orientation and local analysis when the notation, direction, boundary, and level of detail are declared.

### Analysis and application

Define start and end points, actions with verb-object labels, decisions as questions, explicit branch conditions, and connectors that do not cross ambiguously. Add exceptions and feedback loops where they matter.

### Quality checks and limitations

A visually neat flowchart can still be wrong. Validate it against observation, records, variants, and the people who perform and receive the work.

## BA-04-C02-T02 - Swimlanes

### Concept

A swimlane map groups activities by role, team, organization, system, or another responsibility dimension. Movement between lanes exposes handoffs, queues, unclear ownership, and coordination risk.

### Analysis and application

Choose one lane meaning and keep it consistent. Place work in the lane of the responsible actor, not the system screen used. Represent messages, artifacts, wait states, and exceptions where they affect accountability.

### Quality checks and limitations

Do not use lanes to reproduce the organization chart or imply performance blame. Validate shared work, approvals, delegated authority, and external participants.

## BA-04-C02-T03 - SIPOC

### Concept

SIPOC summarizes Suppliers, Inputs, Process, Outputs, and Customers at a high level. It is a scoping and alignment model, not a detailed sequence map.

### Analysis and application

Begin with the process purpose and five to seven major steps, then identify outputs and customers before confirming inputs and suppliers. The customer may be internal, external, regulatory, or downstream; suppliers can include systems and partners.

### Quality checks and limitations

Keep categories distinct and identify quality-critical inputs and outputs. Do not mistake a SIPOC row order for workflow timing or use it to hide alternate outcomes.

## BA-04-C02-T04 - Value Streams

### Concept

A value stream is the end-to-end sequence required to deliver value to a customer or realize an outcome. Value-stream mapping examines both work and information flow, including waiting and inventory.

### Analysis and application

Define the customer and value, product or service family, demand, process steps, information signals, lead and processing times, queues, quality, and constraints. Separate value-creating, necessary non-value-creating, and avoidable work with stakeholder and regulatory context.

### Quality checks and limitations

Do not label controls or accessibility work as waste merely because customers do not see it. Validate time data and distinguish a diagnostic current-state map from an aspirational future state.

## BA-04-C02-T05 - Current State

### Concept

A current-state map is an evidence-based representation of how work operates now, including variants, workarounds, queues, exceptions, and control gaps. It is not the official procedure copied into diagram form.

### Analysis and application

Use interviews, observation, documents, event data, and samples. Record scope, date, volume, variant, confidence, and known gaps. Separate normal variation from noncompliance and explain why workarounds exist before removing them.

### Quality checks and limitations

Confirm the map with multiple perspectives and preserve disagreement. A current-state model is time-bound and must not be presented as universal fact.

## BA-04-C02-T06 - Future State

### Concept

A future-state map represents a proposed operating arrangement designed to improve outcomes within constraints. It is a hypothesis that requires feasibility, impact, control, and transition analysis.

### Analysis and application

Trace each material change to a need and measure. Define new roles, rules, data, systems, controls, exceptions, capacity, and adoption needs. Compare it explicitly with the current state and include a rollback or adjustment route where uncertainty is material.

### Quality checks and limitations

Reject future states that remove visible steps without reallocating responsibility, assume perfect data, or automate unresolved policy. Secure authorization before treating the model as committed scope.

## Integrated process-analysis model

Begin with the need, customer, outcome, and boundary. Establish the current state from multiple sources, select a representation appropriate to the decision, and retain assumptions and variants. Define measures before proposing change. Analyze constraints, handoffs, rework, risks, controls, data, and affected groups. Design a future state as a governed hypothesis, then trace transition and monitoring requirements to authorized outcomes.

## Common misconceptions

- A process is not the same as a department, procedure, system, or diagram.
- A model is a selective representation, not unquestionable operational truth.
- Detail is not quality unless it supports the intended decision.
- Local speed or utilization does not prove better end-to-end performance.
- Automation and standardization do not remove ownership or exceptions.

## Ethics, controls, and professional boundaries

Process change redistributes effort, delay, access, discretion, monitoring, and risk. Include workers, customers, disabled people, external parties, and indirectly affected groups as relevant. Protect personal and operationally sensitive data, distinguish observation from judgment, and preserve dissent. Analysts structure evidence and decisions but do not replace legal, safety, security, privacy, accessibility, statistical, or workforce specialists.

## Chapter overview

Requirements documentation becomes trustworthy through review, traceability, authority, controlled state, and maintained agreement across representations. A polished BRD, PRD, or FRD can still be incomplete, contradictory, infeasible, or disconnected from evidence. Quality control examines meaning and lifecycle, not only grammar and template compliance.

This chapter defines a connected assurance system for BRD, PRD, FRD, models, rules, backlogs, tests, and decisions. It treats automated checks as useful evidence but not independent acceptance. The author may prepare a draft for review; authorized reviewers and the owner determine whether it is accepted for a stated purpose.

## Learning outcomes

The learner should be able to:

- plan completeness and coverage review around purpose, scope, models, and risk;
- detect contradiction across text, tables, diagrams, versions, and external authorities;
- improve clarity, atomicity, and interpretability without unnecessary verbosity;
- judge feasibility, verification, acceptance, and readiness as distinct questions;
- design a typed, bidirectional requirements traceability matrix;
- maintain BRD-to-PRD-to-FRD alignment without uncontrolled duplication;
- govern review comments, conflict decisions, approval, and sign-off evidence;
- manage impact, baselines, audit history, supersession, and retirement.

## BA-03-C11-T01 - Completeness and Coverage Review

### Purpose-relative completeness

Completeness means enough necessary information and coverage for an intended decision at a lifecycle point. A discovery brief can be complete for authorizing research while incomplete for supplier build. A BRD baseline needs outcome, scope, needs, rules, conditions, benefits, and authority; a PRD needs product decisions, user evidence, capability scope, acceptance, and release context; an FRD needs precise behavior, data, interfaces, failure, quality, and verification.

Start by declaring review purpose, artifact state, scope, applicable standard, and material risks. Counted headings do not prove content. A section can exist while containing generic or contradictory text. Review evidence, definitions, boundaries, applicability, ownership, and open decisions.

### Coverage lenses

Vertical coverage traces need to business, stakeholder, product, functional, quality, and evidence layers. Horizontal coverage examines actors, events, processes, rules, data, interfaces, qualities, exceptions, and affected groups. Lifecycle coverage examines creation, change, correction, retention, retirement, migration, operation, and recovery. Governance coverage examines source, owner, state, priority, verification, approval, and history.

Use context, process, journey, state, data, decision, threat, and trace models because each reveals different gaps. Check negative and failure space without attempting infinite enumeration. Apply risk to select rare but consequential scenarios.

### Worked example - complete happy path, missing service

An appointment FRD specifies digital cancellation thoroughly. Coverage review finds no representative authority, assisted channel, notification failure, data retention, concurrent check-in, or operational reconciliation. The screen behavior was detailed but the service was incomplete. The team adds linked requirements and evidence rather than expanding the interface prose.

## BA-03-C11-T02 - Consistency and Contradiction Detection

### Types of consistency

Internal consistency applies within an artifact. External consistency compares policy, law, contract, strategy, architecture, operations, and authoritative definitions. Vertical consistency asks whether lower detail preserves higher intent. Horizontal consistency examines sibling items and interfaces. Temporal consistency distinguishes versions and effective dates. Model consistency compares prose, tables, diagrams, examples, prototypes, schemas, tests, and code-derived evidence.

Uniform words do not guarantee consistent meaning. "Customer" can denote account holder in one section and service recipient in another. Different statements can both be correct when jurisdiction, product, role, or date differs. Applicability should explain the variation rather than forcing false uniformity.

### Detection and resolution

Use governed glossaries, rule catalogues, source comparisons, version diffs, model validation, interface tests, trace queries, and cross-functional walkthroughs. Automated checks find number mismatches, duplicate IDs, undefined terms, broken links, conflicting modals, or incompatible schemas. Reviewers determine semantic conflict and authority.

When sources disagree, record each claim, applicability, authority, version, consequence, and decision. Do not silently choose the newest file or most senior speaker. Resolve in the authoritative source and propagate controlled views. A local edit can create downstream contradiction, so impact analysis follows change.

### Worked example - valid difference or conflict?

A BRD says records remain six years; a PRD says profiles delete after two. Review distinguishes transaction evidence from optional preference profiles and identifies the events starting each period. Both can be valid after data categories and sources are explicit. If both referred to the same record, the policy owner would decide and every linked artifact would change.

## BA-03-C11-T03 - Clarity, Ambiguity, and Atomicity

### Interpretability

A requirement is clear when intended readers derive one materially consistent interpretation. Ambiguity can be lexical, grammatical, referential, quantitative, temporal, boundary, authority, model, or contextual. Look for vague qualifiers, weak modals, passive responsibility, hidden conjunctions, undefined acronyms, missing units, unclear pronouns, and inconsistent terms.

Interpretation testing is stronger than proofreading. Ask reviewers to paraphrase, identify actor and boundary, and provide one conforming and one nonconforming example. Differences show where definitions, models, conditions, or decisions are missing. Plain language improves access, but oversimplification can erase necessary precision.

### Atomicity

Split a statement when obligations have different source, owner, priority, applicability, verification, or change lifecycle. Keep a coherent invariant together when separation would permit partial acceptance that violates the requirement. "Validate and save" may need separation if validation can pass while storage fails; an atomic transaction requirement may deliberately bind related effects.

Titles, rationale, notes, examples, and normative statements should be distinguished. Use one local modal convention. Examples clarify boundaries but should not quietly override a rule. A controlled glossary gives terms stable identity and ownership.

### Failure analysis - an elegant compound statement

"The system shall quickly verify users and securely notify them" combines identity, timing, security, recipient, event, content, and delivery. Review decomposes it into governed identity behavior, measurable response, notification trigger, authorized channel, content classification, delivery status, and recovery. The result is longer but each decision is visible and testable.

## BA-03-C11-T04 - Feasibility, Testability, and Acceptance Readiness

### Different questions

Feasibility asks whether the requirement can be realized within technology, policy, resources, time, skill, supplier, operations, and constraints. Testability asks whether evidence can distinguish conformity from nonconformity. Acceptance readiness asks whether authorized participants have sufficient valid information and evidence to make the relevant decision. A requirement can be testable but infeasible, feasible but ambiguous, or implemented but not acceptable.

Assess feasibility early with architects, engineers, operations, data owners, suppliers, and specialists. Use spikes or prototypes to answer named uncertainties, not to substitute implementation output for requirement. Estimates include migration, data quality, assurance, support, training, and maintenance, not coding alone.

### Verification and acceptance

Assign a method such as test, inspection, analysis, demonstration, measurement, or review. Define object, environment, data, condition, result, tolerance, source, and evaluator. Statistical claims need population, sample, confidence, and decision threshold. Qualitative outcomes can use an agreed rubric and independent review without pretending to be exact measures.

Acceptance criteria protect stakeholder and product intent; Definition of Done supplies recurring delivery controls; release readiness includes operations and dependencies; benefit realization occurs later. Do not collapse these decisions into one Approved label. ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148 addresses quality and lifecycle treatment of requirements information [1].

### Worked example - feasible feature, unready release

Cancellation behavior passes functional tests and is technically feasible. Release remains unready because delegated authority is unresolved, assisted support lacks access, migration reconciliation failed, and notification monitoring has no owner. The product increment can remain built but disabled while the missing readiness conditions are addressed.

## BA-03-C11-T05 - Requirements Traceability Matrix

### Purpose and model

A Requirements Traceability Matrix is a view of typed relationships among controlled items. It may connect evidence, objectives, needs, business requirements, product capabilities, functional and quality requirements, rules, designs, implementation, tests, releases, controls, and measured outcomes. The matrix supports coverage, impact, assurance, scope, and audit.

Each relationship needs direction and meaning: derives from, refines, satisfies, implements, verifies, depends on, conflicts with, supersedes, or measures. Adjacent IDs without relationship semantics create a decorative matrix. Many-to-many relationships are normal. Stable IDs and versions prevent row order or wording changes from breaking identity.

### Design and governance

Define mandatory links by item type and lifecycle stage. Critical controls may require full source-to-evidence trace; exploratory items may need less. Include source and target IDs, types, versions, relationship, rationale where non-obvious, status, owner, and validation result. Generate document views where possible rather than copying links manually.

Automated coverage can flag orphans and invalid references. It cannot prove that the relationship is true. Sample semantic links and investigate suspicious perfect coverage. Excessive traceability becomes stale and costly; proportionality follows consequence and change need.

### Worked example - useful RTM fragment

| Source | Relationship | Target | Review question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business outcome: reduce avoidable no-shows | refined by | Product capability: cancellation and capacity reuse | Does the capability plausibly affect the outcome? |
| Product capability: cancellation | specified by | FR-APT-021 idempotent cancellation | Does the function preserve capability intent? |
| FR-APT-021 | verified by | CT-CANCEL-04 duplicate retry | Does the case test the exact governed version? |
| Privacy rule: minimal notification content | constrains | Interface: notification command | Is prohibited content absent across channels? |

The matrix exposes a capability with no outcome or a test with no requirement. Reviewers still inspect whether the chosen test provides meaningful evidence.

## BA-03-C11-T06 - BRD-to-PRD-to-FRD Cross-document Alignment

### Controlled derivation

BRD, PRD, and FRD answer different questions. The BRD governs business justification, outcomes, scope, needs, rules, benefits, and business authority. The PRD governs product users, value, capability choices, priorities, experience, metrics, and release intent. The FRD governs precise system behavior, data, interfaces, state, failure, quality links, and verification.

Alignment does not mean repeating the same paragraph. Each lower layer refines authorized intent while preserving upward rationale. One business requirement may produce several capabilities; one capability may need several functional and quality requirements. A shared rule or definition should have one authoritative home and references from each view.

### Change and conflict

Review boundaries, terminology, populations, measures, rules, exclusions, and assumptions across layers. Product discovery may reveal that a BRD assumption is false; functional analysis may reveal infeasibility; tests may expose an ambiguous rule. Feedback travels upward through controlled change instead of silently modifying only the lower document.

Avoid forced one-to-one mapping. Use typed links and coverage rules. An FRD function without product or business justification may be enabling work, necessary design, or scope drift. A business outcome without product capability is unrealized. A PRD feature contradicted by the BRD's out-of-scope boundary needs decision.

### Worked example - alignment prevents feature drift

The BRD outcome is equitable appointment completion. The PRD proposes cancellation and rescheduling; the FRD begins specifying loyalty points copied from another product. Trace review finds no authorized source. The points are removed from committed scope. Later discovery can propose them through impact and ethics review rather than allowing implementation enthusiasm to redefine the change.

## BA-03-C11-T07 - Review Comments, Conflict Resolution, and Sign-off

### Structured review

Plan reviews by question and role. Business owners examine value and scope; users and operations examine context and consequences; product participants examine choices and priorities; engineers examine feasibility; testers examine observability; specialists examine security, privacy, accessibility, safety, legal, and compliance; configuration owners examine identity and history.

Provide the exact controlled version, review scope, definitions, models, known issues, decision deadline, and accessible method. Workshops support shared interpretation but should not silence participants who need individual review. Absence of response is not approval.

### Comment and conflict lifecycle

A comment records identifier, artifact item, concern, consequence, evidence, proposal, originator, owner, state, disposition, rationale, and affected revision. Valid dispositions include corrected, accepted as is with rationale, deferred with condition, duplicate, outside current scope with owner, and referred for decision. "Closed" alone is not informative.

Conflicts identify the competing needs or sources, applicability, options, consequences, decision authority, and outcome. The analyst can recommend but should not invent authority. Record dissent relevant to risk. Sign-off identifies exact version, purpose, scope, approver identity and authority, date, conditions, exceptions, and accepted residual risk.

### Worked example - conditional sign-off

The sponsor approves a pilot BRD and PRD. Security approves the threat treatment; accessibility review requires correction of identity recovery; operations accepts a capacity assumption only for one center. The baseline records those conditions. Product discovery continues, but release cannot expand until evidence satisfies the named conditions.

## BA-03-C11-T08 - Change Impact, Baselines, Audit Trail, and Document Retirement

### Lifecycle control

A baseline is an approved reference configuration for a purpose. It identifies exact items and versions, decision, authority, date, applicability, and accepted conditions. A baseline permits controlled change; it does not freeze learning. Different lifecycle decisions can have different baselines.

A change request states source, rationale, desired outcome, affected items, urgency, alternatives, impact, risk, effective date, and evidence. Analysis traverses trace relationships and applies expert review across users, process, data, rules, interfaces, quality, tests, operations, contracts, training, benefits, and documents. Include the consequence of no change.

### Audit and retirement

The audit trail preserves who changed what, when, why, under which authority, from which version, and with what result. Corrections create history rather than erasure. Workflow logs alone are not sufficient if they cannot reconstruct content and decision. Access and confidentiality protect sensitive review evidence.

Retirement marks an item inactive when no active dependency requires it and retention obligations permit. Identify successor, effective point, consumer migration, data disposition, archive, and communication. Do not delete IDs or historical requirements that explain existing cases. NASA systems guidance connects requirements management with configuration control and lifecycle integrity [2].

### Worked example - retiring an interface version

An old cancellation API remains used by one center and two reports. The retirement plan inventories consumers, provides a compatible replacement, migrates and verifies them, monitors residual traffic, archives the contract and tests, and disables access after approval. Historical requests retain the version used. Retirement evidence closes the change rather than relying on a deprecation email.

## Integrated quality and governance model

Quality control is a feedback system. Coverage review finds missing information; consistency review reveals conflicting meaning; clarity analysis reveals interpretation; feasibility and verification expose impractical or unobservable statements; traceability connects intent and evidence; cross-document alignment preserves level; review resolves issues through authority; configuration control maintains integrity over time.

| Review dimension | Defect revealed | Control response |
|---|---|---|
| Completeness | Missing actor, lifecycle, rule, failure or evidence | Add or explicitly govern the gap |
| Consistency | Contradictory meaning, value, scope or version | Establish applicability and authoritative decision |
| Clarity | Multiple reasonable interpretations | Define, model, split, quantify or exemplify |
| Feasibility | Unrealizable obligation or hidden lifecycle effort | Compare options, constraints, evidence and risk |
| Testability | No feasible discriminating evidence | Refine condition, method, measure or boundary |
| Traceability | Orphan intent, behavior, implementation or test | Add valid typed link or remove unjustified scope |
| Alignment | BRD, PRD and FRD refine different intentions | Correct source and propagate controlled change |
| Governance | Unknown version, authority, decision or history | Baseline, record, approve, change or retire |

### End-to-end assurance example

A release candidate for appointment cancellation has complete-looking documents. The governance review finds that the BRD population includes representatives, the PRD user model omits them, and the FRD permission matrix denies them. Tests pass because only customer accounts are used. Cross-document trace and scenario coverage reveal the gap. The authority confirms delegation policy; PRD, FRD, tests, support, privacy evidence, and release scope change together.

The corrected package undergoes role-specific review. Automated gates confirm IDs, references, density, examples, and structural conditions. Semantic review checks meaning and evidence. Specialist reviewers examine controls. The owner approves the exact baseline with one monitored capacity assumption. Authoring checks are retained as evidence but are not mislabeled as acceptance.

## Advanced practice

### Quality economics

The purpose of governance is reliable decisions, not maximum ceremony. Defects found before supplier commitment, data migration, or release are usually cheaper than escaped defects, but review also has cost. Apply stronger assurance to high-consequence, shared, long-lived, regulated, hard-to-reverse, or externally contracted requirements. Use lighter controls for reversible exploratory work while preserving source and decision.

Risk-based sampling can complement exhaustive structural checks. Automatically validate every ID and link, then semantically inspect critical chains and samples. Review high-volatility areas after changes. Track escaped defect patterns to improve review methods rather than measuring success by low comment counts.

### Tooling and automation

Requirements tools can enforce attributes, workflows, permissions, links, versions, queries, and views. Git can preserve text history and review. Spreadsheets can support small trace matrices. None determines whether a requirement is true, valuable, or authorized. Select tools based on scale, relationships, audit, collaboration, integration, access, and longevity.

Automated gates should have calibrated severity and clear scope. A blocking gate represents a property whose absence disqualifies the artifact for the stated stage. An advisory gate exposes improvement without falsely failing a benchmark that never met it. New and rebuilt content can adopt stronger standards than legacy content under an explicit remediation plan.

Generative AI can compare versions, suggest ambiguity, summarize comments, or propose traces. It can also fabricate sources, create convincing repetition, leak confidential evidence, and normalize contradictions. Human reviewers verify source, meaning, authority, and consequence. Machine output remains a draft until reviewed.

### Publication integrity

Working chapter files retain module, chapter, and topic codes for precise review. The final learner-facing module removes those internal codes and workflow stamps while retaining readable titles, references, and teaching. Both forms are generated from the same controlled source. A manually edited publication would become an uncontrolled parallel version.

Typography and navigation form part of usability. The governed publication hierarchy uses module title 64 pt, chapter title 32 pt, topic title 24 pt, and subtopic title 16 pt. Font size cannot correct repeated or confused content; semantic review remains separate from visual quality assurance.

### Review architecture by risk

Build a review architecture from the decisions and harms the artifact can create. A business sponsor cannot substitute for a privacy specialist, and a security reviewer cannot decide product value. Assign review questions, artifacts, evidence, and authority to roles. Where one person holds several roles, record which responsibility they exercise for each decision.

Entry criteria prevent premature ceremony. The artifact should identify purpose, version, scope, definitions, unresolved questions, and known limitations before formal review. Reviewers need time, access, and an accessible format. Exit criteria state which defects must be corrected, which may be accepted with authority, and what evidence is needed. A review meeting is one method, not the status itself.

The IIBA standard treats requirements verification, validation, lifecycle management, and stakeholder collaboration as distinct but connected responsibilities [3]. That distinction supports layered review: verify clarity and consistency, validate alignment and value, assess feasibility and risk, then authorize an applicable baseline. Combining every question into one sign-off invites reviewers to assume someone else checked it.

### Worked example - calibrated review for two artifacts

A reversible internal notification preference uses peer analysis, product-owner decision, accessibility check, automated tests, and monitored release. A benefits eligibility rule uses policy authority, legal and equality review, security and privacy review, independent decision-table verification, UAT, configuration audit, and formal baseline. Both are controlled; their assurance differs because consequence, reversibility, and authority differ.

### Cross-reference and vocabulary governance

Cross-references are requirements information. A link to the wrong module title, retired rule, or superseded interface can direct a learner or delivery team to incompatible meaning. Validate target identity, title, state, and applicability. Human-readable link text should remain understandable while stable IDs carry precision in working records.

A glossary is a governed set of concepts, not a list of acronyms. Each term has definition, scope, owner, synonyms or prohibited alternatives, source, and version. Definitions can vary legitimately by context, but the applicability must be explicit. A global definition should not overwrite a legally distinct local one. Models and data dictionaries reference the same concept where possible.

Vocabulary review looks for near-synonyms, overloaded terms, undefined capitals, and accidental abbreviations. In this curriculum PRD means Product Requirements Document; PDR is not used as an alternative because it commonly denotes other activities. Naming clarity prevents a small editorial variation from creating a document-selection error.

### Audit trail reconstruction

A useful audit trail can reconstruct content and decision, not only workflow clicks. It shows previous and new values, actor, timestamp, rationale, authority, source request, affected baseline, review, and resulting release. System administrators need controlled access but should not be able to alter history without evidence. Clock, identity, and retention controls matter when records support assurance.

Reconstruction tests quality. Select a material change and ask an independent reviewer to reproduce why it occurred, which requirements and tests changed, who approved it, when it became effective, and which cases remained under the earlier state. Missing answers reveal a history or applicability gap. Keep access proportionate because the trail may contain personal, legal, security, or commercial information.

### Worked example - approval without content integrity

An electronic workflow records sponsor approval, but the attached PDF can be replaced under the same filename. The approval proves identity and time but not content. Governance adds an immutable version or hash, artifact manifest, controlled storage, and a link between the decision and exact content. Later viewers can verify what was approved rather than trust a mutable label.

### Quality of trace relationships

Trace validation has syntactic and semantic layers. Syntactic checks confirm source and target exist, versions are valid, relationship type is permitted, and required links are present. Semantic review asks whether the lower item truly refines or verifies the upper item. A login test does not verify a cancellation rule merely because both belong to the same feature.

Relationship rationale is useful when the link is indirect. A performance requirement may constrain several use cases; a data-retention rule may govern multiple stores and reports. Graph queries can reveal high-impact hubs and fragile orphan chains. They can also reveal suspicious uniformity, such as every requirement linking one-to-one to one test regardless of complexity.

Trace matrices should expose status without becoming the source of truth. Generate them from governed relationships where feasible. If a spreadsheet is authoritative, control identifiers, validation, ownership, version, and review. Sorting or inserting rows must not alter identity.

### Change classification

Classify changes by effect. Editorial correction improves presentation without altering meaning. Clarification removes ambiguity while preserving intent. Elaboration adds authorized detail within a boundary. Correction fixes an erroneous requirement. Scope change alters commitment. Policy or regulatory change alters authority. Emergency containment introduces temporary behavior. Each class can have different review and approval thresholds.

Classification itself needs review when consequence is uncertain. Calling a threshold change "clarification" does not make it one. Compare old and new interpretations, affected users, implementation, tests, and benefits. If an existing conforming solution would become nonconforming, meaning probably changed. Record transition and effective point.

Temporary controls have owners, expiry, monitoring, and retirement. Emergency work often becomes permanent because the organization restores service and loses urgency. Keep the change open until the permanent resolution or accepted long-term decision is complete.

### Worked example - a clarification that changes scope

A requirement says notification is available to customers. A proposed edit replaces customers with customers and representatives. The author calls it clarification. Authorization, privacy, identity, content, support, and tests all change; the earlier implementation was conforming for customers only. Impact evidence shows a product and functional scope change, so the appropriate owners review and baseline it.

### Accessible and inclusive review

Review processes themselves must be usable. Large unstructured documents, image-only diagrams, short synchronous meetings, jargon, and inaccessible tooling exclude valuable reviewers. Provide navigable headings, text alternatives, readable tables, enough time, asynchronous routes, and assistance. The UK Service Standard emphasizes accessibility and multidisciplinary collaboration across service work [4].

Power affects comment disposition. A frontline or user concern should not be downgraded because it lacks executive vocabulary. Record the consequence and evidence, seek clarification, and route the decision correctly. Conversely, participation does not mean every preference becomes a requirement. Transparent authority and rationale protect both inclusion and scope.

Review evidence should state who was represented and who was absent. Do not claim universal user validation from a narrow sample. When direct participation is infeasible, record proxies and limitations and plan later evidence. Independent review is a control precisely because authors can be blind to assumptions that feel obvious within their own context.

### Governance maturity

Maturity is not the number of templates or approvals. It is the organization's ability to identify current applicable information, connect it to evidence and outcomes, detect defects, make authorized decisions, propagate change, and learn from results. A simple disciplined repository can outperform an expensive tool full of stale records.

Improvement should follow observed failure. If teams repeatedly ship orphan functionality, strengthen upward trace and scope review. If policy changes miss reports, improve lineage and impact analysis. If review takes months without finding defects, simplify authority or review design. Measures serve learning rather than performance theater.

The final governance question is whether a reasonable authorized person can rely on the information for the stated decision while seeing its uncertainty and limits. Passing structure, density, citation, and similarity gates supports that judgment. It never replaces independent semantic, specialist, or owner review.

At module scale, governance also prevents chapter boundaries from becoming duplication. Requirements concepts, elicitation, acceptance, priority, lifecycle, digital concerns, BRD, PRD, and FRD should reinforce one another while answering different questions. Repeated definitions use controlled cross-references; chapter-specific explanations apply the concept at the new decision level. Semantic similarity review detects suspicious reuse, while editorial review determines whether intentional recap is proportionate.

A collated publication needs its own integrity check. Removing internal codes must not remove meaningful headings, sources, examples, tables, or distinctions. Chapter order, contents, page numbers, running headers, and references should guide learners through the requirements chain. Rendered-page inspection catches clipping and hierarchy defects that source validation cannot see.

The accepted source remains the authority for every generated view and later correction.

Always.

## Common misconceptions

- A completed template is not proof of completeness.
- Consistent formatting is not semantic consistency.
- Testable does not mean valuable or feasible.
- A full RTM can contain invalid relationships.
- BRD, PRD, and FRD should align, not duplicate each other.
- Closing a comment is not the same as resolving its concern.
- Approval applies to a version and purpose, not all future use.
- Retirement preserves necessary history instead of deleting it.

## Ethics, limitations, and professional boundaries

Governance can suppress dissent when senior approval, inaccessible review, or short deadlines are treated as truth. Give affected participants usable review methods, record material disagreement, and distinguish silence from consent. Protect personal, security-sensitive, legal, and commercial evidence while ensuring authorized reviewers see relevant risk.

The analyst structures assurance but does not self-accept authored content or assume specialist authority. Owners decide within delegated scope. Independent review can find semantic and ethical defects that automated gates miss. A green tool result is evidence about measured properties, not a claim that the curriculum or requirement is correct.

## Chapter overview

Requirements capability develops through increasingly connected decisions, not by filling three templates. This chapter explains how a learner should approach six controlled documentation projects: an introductory BRD, an intermediate PRD, an advanced FRD, an integrated document pack, a repair exercise, and a professional defense and handover. It provides theory, workflow, review logic, and quality standards; the actual submissions and grading belong to later production phases.

All projects should use fictional or authorized evidence. Do not copy confidential business documents, personal data, proprietary interfaces, security details, or real credentials into learning artifacts. When information is intentionally incomplete, label the assumption and show how a professional analyst would seek evidence or authority.

## Learning outcomes

The learner should be able to:

- plan a BRD project from evidence to controlled business baseline;
- derive a PRD from business intent and user research without copying sections;
- specify an FRD with precise behavior, data, decisions, state, failure, and evidence;
- maintain one traceable, aligned BRD-PRD-FRD documentation pack;
- diagnose and repair a poor corpus through root-cause analysis rather than cosmetic editing;
- present, defend, approve, hand over, maintain, and retire requirements professionally.

## BA-03-C12-T01 - Beginner BRD Project

### Project purpose and evidence pack

The beginner project teaches the movement from evidence to a business decision. Use a bounded service problem such as missed appointments, delayed invoice approval, repeated address-change failure, or inaccessible booking. The evidence pack should contain a short strategy or policy excerpt, service measures, stakeholder observations, current-state description, constraints, and deliberately unresolved questions.

Begin by inventorying claims and sources. Separate observed fact, interpretation, assumption, preference, decision, and proposed solution. Define vocabulary and population. Frame the problem or opportunity without embedding a feature. Identify who owns the business outcome, scope, rules, benefits, and baseline.

### Construction sequence

Build the causal chain: context and evidence; problem and consequence; objectives and guardrails; stakeholder needs; current-state causes; option space; business capability and requirements; scope and exclusions; assumptions, constraints, dependencies, and risks; benefits and KPIs; review and approval. Write the executive summary last as a faithful decision view.

The first BRD should be concise but controlled. Include stable IDs in the working source, trace each major requirement to evidence and outcome, and show at least one conflict requiring authority. A current-state model and boundary model normally reveal more than narrative alone. Do not add product features merely to make the document look substantial.

### Worked example - from request to BRD

The fictional sponsor requests SMS reminders to reduce no-shows. Evidence shows lead time, inaccessible cancellation, and incorrect contact preference contribute more than forgetfulness. The learner writes an outcome-based BRD that includes confirmation, cancellation, capacity reuse, assisted service, and data quality while keeping channel design open. The sponsor can authorize a business boundary rather than a predetermined message feature.

### Review standard

A credible submission lets a reviewer state why change is needed, who is affected, what outcome and guardrails matter, what is in and out, which business conditions are required, what remains uncertain, and who decides. Reject a BRD whose metrics have no denominator, whose scope cannot achieve its objective, or whose requirements begin as detailed system design.

## BA-03-C12-T02 - Intermediate PRD Project

### Derive rather than duplicate

The PRD project begins from an accepted BRD baseline and a user-research pack. The learner identifies which business outcome the product can influence, relevant users and affected non-users, jobs and contexts, evidence strength, capability options, dependencies, and product authority. Any discovery finding that contradicts the BRD becomes a controlled feedback item.

Represent users through evidence, not imaginative persona detail. Include successful, failed, assisted, abandoned, accessibility, and recovery contexts. Frame problems and use cases before features. Define the product vision, value proposition, capability boundary, non-goals, first safe slice, priorities, and roadmap assumptions.

### Acceptance and measurement

Decompose capabilities into epics, stories, enablers, models, and cross-cutting requirements without making the backlog the sole source of knowledge. Define item-specific acceptance and a shared Definition of Done. Include experience, content, accessibility, privacy, analytics, experiment, release, support, and rollback requirements according to risk.

Product measures should identify decision, population, event, calculation, segment, source, owner, and guardrails. An experiment names hypothesis and decision rule. User engagement, feature delivery, and outcome improvement are different kinds of evidence. The UK Service Standard emphasizes user needs, accessibility, multidisciplinary work, and reliable service rather than interface delivery alone [1].

### Worked example - product choice under uncertainty

The accepted business outcome is equitable appointment completion. Research shows users need authoritative status and recoverable cancellation; policy for automatic rescheduling remains inconsistent. The learner scopes viewing and cancellation first, places rescheduling behind a rule-validation dependency, retains assisted continuity, and defines outcome and burden measures. The PRD demonstrates product judgment rather than copying the BRD's problem section.

### Review standard

Reviewers should be able to explain for whom the product creates value, which evidence supports that claim, what it will and will not do, why the sequence is defensible, how users act and recover, how acceptable behavior is judged, and what evidence controls expansion. Reject feature lists with fictional personas, vanity metrics, or an MVP that postpones required safety and accessibility.

## BA-03-C12-T03 - Advanced FRD Project

### Functional system model

The advanced project takes one accepted product capability and specifies a bounded service or system. Begin with context, trust boundaries, actors, goals, authoritative systems, events, data, and dependencies. Build typed functional decomposition and stable requirement IDs. Link every function upward to product and business intent.

Specify actor authorization, inputs, processing, outputs, validation, data semantics, interfaces, rules, calculations, states, transitions, alternate behavior, error, recovery, qualities, and specialist controls. Use the representation that exposes each decision: prose, context diagram, use case, data dictionary, decision table, state model, interface contract, quality scenario, or example.

### Verification and execution discipline

Each requirement identifies a verification method and links to planned evidence. Expected results come from authorized rules and models, never from copying whatever an implementation produced. Critical calculations should be independently reproduced. Interface examples should be validated against their schema where tooling exists. UAT scenarios assess realistic user and business need but do not replace system and specialist tests.

ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148 provides lifecycle guidance for requirements engineering and information items [2]. The learner applies the principles proportionately rather than copying standard headings. Unknown policy, data, or interface detail remains a controlled TBD with owner and consequence; it is not invented to complete the document.

### Worked example - cancellation FRD

The learner specifies authorized, idempotent cancellation. The decision table covers state, delegation, cutoff, closure, and protected service. The state model addresses concurrent check-in. The API handles timeout through status query. Data requirements define center time and rule version. Security, privacy, accessibility, performance, audit, notification failure, reconciliation, and tests connect through trace links.

### Review standard

An independent reviewer should navigate from any functional item to source, rule, data, quality, test, and intended outcome. Reject design-heavy documents that constrain implementation without authority, happy paths with no recovery, role lists without object-level permissions, and schemas with undefined business meaning.

## BA-03-C12-T04 - Integrated BRD-PRD-FRD Documentation Pack

### One controlled information architecture

The integrated project demonstrates alignment across three decision layers. The BRD owns business context, outcome, scope, needs, rules, benefits, and business baseline. The PRD owns product users, value, capabilities, priority, experience, measurement, and release intent. The FRD owns precise behavior, data, interfaces, decisions, state, failure, quality links, and verification.

Do not paste the same requirement into all documents. Give each item one authoritative home and use typed relationships. Shared definitions and rules are governed once. Generated views can show context, but a change must propagate from the source. A requirements traceability matrix supports navigation and coverage without becoming a decorative spreadsheet.

### Integration workflow

Baseline the BRD for the selected scope. Derive product outcomes and capability choices, then baseline the PRD decision. Allocate precise behavior to the FRD boundary. Build forward and backward traces. Review vocabulary, populations, boundaries, numbers, measures, rules, applicability, and exclusions across the set. Feed infeasibility or new user evidence upward through controlled change.

| Layer | Owns | Must not become |
|---|---|---|
| BRD | Business justification, outcomes, scope, rules, benefits | Detailed product backlog or system design |
| PRD | User value, capabilities, priorities, experience, metrics, release | Repeated business case or interface specification |
| FRD | Observable behavior, data, decisions, state, failure, evidence | Architecture implementation manual |
| RTM | Typed relationships and coverage | Uncontrolled duplicate source |

### Worked example - one trace chain

The chain starts with reduced avoidable no-shows without unequal exclusion. It refines into authoritative viewing and cancellation, then into idempotent cancellation behavior, eligibility rule, appointment data, notification contract, and recovery. Tests and release evidence link downward; production outcome measures link back upward. A proposed loyalty feature has no source and is removed from the baseline.

### Review standard

The pack should remain coherent when read in either direction. Every committed feature has justification; every approved business outcome has a realization path; every critical function has evidence; every shared rule has one owner. Reject one-to-one trace manufactured for appearance, competing copies, and lower-level change that silently alters business intent.

## BA-03-C12-T05 - Repair and Rationalize a Poor Documentation Set

### Diagnose before editing

The repair project begins with a deliberately defective corpus: repeated boilerplate, vague requirements, missing codes, wrong cross-references, duplicate rules, conflicting titles, unsupported metrics, design masquerading as need, stale approval, orphan tests, and uncontrolled copies. The learner measures and classifies defects before rewriting.

Distinguish structural, semantic, evidence, trace, governance, integrity, and publication defects. Determine scope and consequence. A fixed answer-key pattern or fabricated evidence can disqualify an assessment or document rather than invite local correction. Repeated template text may require rebuilding because there is too little genuine content to retain. Authentic under-depth prose can be deepened in place.

### Root-cause correction

Correct the source of truth first. Restore stable identity, establish one current version, resolve title and vocabulary authority, and quarantine misleading publications. Rebuild or deepen one controlled chapter or artifact at a time. Apply measurable gates, semantic similarity review, source verification, and independent review. Do not mark author self-checks as acceptance.

Automated gates need calibrated severity. A gate that claims to block but exits successfully on failure is itself defective. Benchmark new rules against known good and bad content. Test file selection so budgets and checkpoints are not graded as chapters. Preserve rejected artifacts when needed for audit while preventing learners from mistaking them for current publications.

### Worked example - fixing apparent completeness

A corpus has every topic row marked Covered and every chapter stamped Self Audit Passed. Measurement finds repeated skeleton paragraphs, no examples, and density below the controlled floor. The learner voids unsupported stamps, registers the issue, quarantines stale PDFs, corrects the audit tool, and selects one calibration chapter. A clean gate result becomes evidence for independent review, not proof of owner acceptance.

### Review standard

The repair report should reproduce claims, explain root cause, protect genuine material, identify affected artifacts, prevent unsafe continuation, and state what remains. Reject cosmetic editing that leaves the process capable of recreating the same defect, or bulk regeneration that destroys valid provenance.

## BA-03-C12-T06 - Present, Defend, Approve, and Hand Over Requirements

### Present for a decision

A professional presentation is organized around the audience's decision: context, evidence, outcome, scope, key requirements, options, trade-offs, risks, uncertainty, recommendation, and request. It does not read every document page. Use clear visuals and examples while preserving links to detailed controlled information.

Defending requirements means explaining evidence, reasoning, boundaries, alternatives, and consequence—not resisting all change. Invite challenge. State which claims are established, inferred, assumed, or disputed. When a reviewer identifies better evidence, update through control. Do not improvise policy or promise scope to win approval.

### Approval and handover

Approval identifies exact version or manifest, purpose, scope, authority, date, conditions, exceptions, and residual risk. Different authorities may approve business baseline, product release, technical readiness, privacy, security, accessibility, and UAT. Silence and meeting attendance are not sign-off.

Handover gives recipients access, navigation, glossary, models, traceability, open decisions, known limitations, support, change process, ownership, and evidence. Demonstrate how to find the current applicable item and reconstruct a decision. Confirm tool permissions and retention. Archive superseded versions without presenting them as current.

NASA systems guidance emphasizes requirements and configuration management across lifecycle control [3]. A handover is therefore a transfer of usable governed knowledge, not an email attachment. Maintenance and retirement responsibilities remain named after delivery.

### Worked example - defending a conditional release

The team presents cancellation readiness. Functional, security, accessibility, reconciliation, support, and pilot evidence pass, but notification delivery for one supplier remains uncertain. The recommendation releases to one center with monitored retry, assisted confirmation, rollback, and a two-week review. The owner approves the exact scope and condition. Handover includes dashboards, runbook, trace view, known limit, and escalation.

### Review standard

A recipient should state what is approved, what is not, what conditions remain, who owns each decision, where authoritative information lives, and how change occurs. Reject presentations that hide material risk, approval captured against mutable files, or handover that depends on the original author's memory.

## Integrated project progression

The sequence grows in complexity and responsibility:

| Project | Primary competency | Main failure to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Beginner BRD | Evidence to business decision | Solution disguised as business need |
| Intermediate PRD | Business intent to product choice | Feature list without user or outcome evidence |
| Advanced FRD | Product capability to precise behavior | Design detail without governed requirement |
| Integrated pack | Cross-level trace and alignment | Repeated competing requirements |
| Repair project | Root-cause diagnosis and controlled remediation | Cosmetic correction or unsafe bulk regeneration |
| Defense and handover | Decision, approval, continuity, and change | Presentation replacing controlled evidence |

### Portfolio evidence

A professional portfolio should preserve final learner-facing documents and a separate controlled working trail showing source inventory, decisions, review comments, trace model, version history, and corrections. Remove personal, confidential, proprietary, and security-sensitive material. State where examples are fictional or pseudocode.

Evaluation should judge reasoning, evidence, distinctions, coverage, traceability, quality, and governance, not page count. A concise artifact can be strong when it supports the decision. Word-density floors protect against empty treatment but do not make length the objective. Repetition, copied templates, and inflated appendices reduce quality.

### Progressive independence

The beginner project can provide structured evidence and a guided boundary. The intermediate project introduces conflicting user and product evidence. The advanced project includes incomplete rules and dependencies that must be governed, not invented. The integrated and repair projects require independent planning and defense.

Feedback should identify exact module, chapter, topic, requirement, model, or trace link in coded working artifacts. The final collated publication hides internal codes for reading. This dual structure supports both precise correction and professional presentation.

### Project control and checkpoints

Each project begins with a short contract: purpose, controlled topics, evidence provided, permitted assumptions, required representations, source and citation rules, review roles, acceptance authority, and expected outputs. A checkpoint records what is complete, which gates passed, what remains unresolved, and the next authorized action. It should prevent a later session from treating an unfinished or rejected artifact as ready.

Use checkpoints at meaningful decisions rather than arbitrary page counts. For the BRD, pause after problem, outcome, and scope framing before detailed requirements. For the PRD, pause after user evidence and capability boundary before backlog decomposition. For the FRD, review context, rules, data, and state before writing extensive test links. Early correction protects genuine work and reduces downstream rework.

Source control preserves changes but does not replace content governance. Commit one accepted authoring unit at a time with a meaningful message. Do not combine mechanical title repair, policy changes, new authorship, and generated publications in one opaque change. Review diffs for accidental deletion of codes, sources, audit state, or user-owned files.

### Evidence-based evaluation rubric

The later assessment design can score several dimensions separately: problem and outcome validity; source handling; boundary quality; stakeholder and authority reasoning; requirement precision; rule and data completeness; scenario and recovery coverage; trace semantics; measurement quality; document alignment; review disposition; and professional communication. A weighted rubric should state what constitutes insufficient, competent, and advanced evidence.

Do not reward length directly. Density floors detect superficial coverage, but evaluation focuses on topic-specific reasoning. Penalize repeated generic paragraphs, unsupported claims, invented authority, contradictory models, and copied expected results. Credit explicit uncertainty and a well-governed question when the case lacks enough evidence for a justified answer.

Calibration uses sample submissions representing clear levels. Independent reviewers apply the rubric and compare judgments. Disagreement can reveal ambiguous criteria or legitimate professional alternatives. Revise the rubric rather than forcing uniformity through hidden preference. Preserve calibration examples without turning them into text learners can copy mechanically.

### Worked example - evaluating a concise strong project

One BRD is shorter than another but identifies the correct problem, cites evidence, separates causes from symptoms, defines a coherent boundary, records a stakeholder conflict, links six business requirements to outcomes, and uses measurable guardrails. The longer submission repeats context, lists features, and declares approval without authority. The rubric rates the concise artifact higher because it supports a defensible decision.

### Iteration after review

Correction begins with the review issue, not a wholesale rewrite. Identify affected topic or item, root cause, related traces, and intended acceptance. Preserve valid prose and history. After revision, rerun structural and semantic gates, recheck sources and cross-references, regenerate affected views, and inspect the latest PDF. A locally corrected paragraph can still make another section inconsistent.

Owner feedback should be captured precisely. "This section is confusing" becomes a discussion to identify conflicting names, repeated purpose, wrong level, missing example, or visual hierarchy. The coded working file enables reference to the exact topic; the code-free publication lets the owner judge learner experience. Both feedback routes return to one controlled source.

Completion means every contracted chapter and topic has substantive coverage, required gates pass, working files retain codes, publication removes them, governance records match actual state, and outputs pass visual inspection. It does not mean the author self-accepts the curriculum. Independent review and owner approval remain visible until explicitly granted.

The project portfolio should also demonstrate improvement across the sequence. Early feedback about solution bias should be visibly applied in the PRD; feedback about ambiguity should improve the FRD; feedback about broken relationships should strengthen the integrated pack. A final reflection cites exact decisions and revisions rather than claiming general growth. This creates evidence of professional judgment, not only finished documents.

## Common misconceptions

- Completing a template is not completing analysis.
- A project can use fictional evidence without inventing unsupported conclusions.
- More pages do not prove better requirements.
- A PRD should derive from, not copy, a BRD.
- An FRD should specify behavior without becoming source code or architecture.
- A full RTM can still contain meaningless links.
- Repair should address root cause and governance, not only prose.
- Defense means reasoned openness to challenge, not refusal to change.
- Handover is incomplete when recipients cannot navigate authority and history.

## Ethics, limitations, and professional boundaries

Learning projects should not expose real personal data, internal vulnerabilities, legal advice, proprietary contracts, or customer information. Use fabricated datasets and clearly fictional organizations unless explicit authorization and controls exist. Avoid stereotypes in personas and scenarios. Include accessibility, privacy, security, and unequal-impact reasoning as product and service concerns, not decorative appendices.

The learner does not assume sponsor, legal, policy, security, accessibility, or risk authority. Where the case lacks an authorized decision, record a question, recommendation, owner, and consequence. A professional artifact makes its limits visible. Generative tools may help draft structure, but evidence, citations, expected results, and approvals must be verified.

## Chapter overview

A Functional Requirements Document specifies observable behavior for a defined system, service, or process boundary. It translates approved business and product intent into actors, functions, inputs, outputs, rules, data, states, interfaces, errors, qualities, and verification relationships. It says what behavior is necessary and under which conditions; architecture and design decide how to realize that behavior unless a justified constraint applies.

The name FRD is not universal. Equivalent information may live in a software requirements specification, use cases, models, interface contracts, rule catalogues, data definitions, quality specifications, and linked backlog. The governing principle is one authoritative home for each item, stable identity, traceability, and controlled change.

The running example is the appointment product introduced in the BRD and PRD chapters. The functional scope includes authoritative appointment viewing and cancellation. Rescheduling remains outside the first release pending policy validation. Examples use concise pseudonotation for teaching; they are not executable production specifications.

## Learning outcomes

The learner should be able to:

- define system context, responsibility, trust boundaries, and external actors;
- decompose functions while preserving business and product traceability;
- write precise, uniquely identified, atomic requirement statements;
- specify permissions, input, processing, output, validation, and audit behavior;
- govern data semantics, interfaces, business rules, calculations, workflows, and state;
- describe alternate, error, and recovery behavior without hiding partial completion;
- connect functional behavior to quality, security, privacy, accessibility, and compliance;
- link requirements to test cases, UAT, implementation, release, and change evidence.

## BA-03-C10-T01 - System Context, Boundaries, and External Actors

### Define the subject

The system boundary names the subject whose required behavior the FRD controls. Everything outside that subject is an actor, dependency, source, consumer, or environment. Boundary decisions allocate responsibility: identity verification may be internal to an end-to-end service but external to an appointment component. Unstated changes cause duplicated or missing behavior.

A context model shows people, organizations, devices, systems, suppliers, channels, data stores, and authorities that exchange information or events with the subject. Label flows with business meaning rather than technology alone. Add trust, jurisdiction, ownership, and lifecycle boundaries where they affect requirements. The model and prose must agree.

### Actors and responsibility

An actor represents a role played by a person or external system. Define goals, permissions, triggers, and relevant context. Avoid one generic User when customer, carer, agent, scheduler, auditor, and identity provider have different responsibilities. An external system can initiate or respond, but the FRD should not assign it behavior outside the contracted interface.

State what the subject guarantees and what it assumes. If an upstream service supplies eligibility, identify contract, freshness, error behavior, and fallback. Assumptions that affect correctness become controlled dependencies or requirements; they do not remain hidden in a diagram note. OMG UML defines actors and use cases relative to a subject boundary [1].

### Worked example - cancellation boundary

The Appointment Service owns eligibility evaluation using an approved rule, cancellation state, immutable reference, notification request, and audit event. Identity and Notification are external services. A Customer or Authorized Representative initiates cancellation; an Agent uses an assisted channel. The service does not guarantee message delivery but records Notification's accepted or failed result and exposes authoritative appointment status.

## BA-03-C10-T02 - Functional Decomposition and Requirement Hierarchy

### From outcome to behavior

Functional decomposition divides a capability into coherent behaviors while preserving its relationship to higher intent. A hierarchy may connect business requirement, product capability, feature, use case, functional group, and atomic requirement. Label relationship types; indentation alone cannot distinguish derives from, part of, depends on, or variant of.

Choose an axis appropriate to the decision. Decompose by actor goal, event, state, process stage, information object, operation, rule, or scenario. Mixing axes inside one tree creates overlap. Many behaviors belong in a graph: audit generation supports several use cases and should not be copied into each as competing requirements.

### Granularity and allocation

An atomic requirement expresses one obligation that can be traced, changed, and verified meaningfully. Atomic does not mean one clause at all costs. A transaction guarantee may require related effects to remain one logical obligation. Split when parts have different source, owner, priority, applicability, or verification; keep together when separation destroys the required invariant.

Allocate behavior to components only when the boundary decision is authorized. The FRD can state that the Appointment Service must prevent duplicate cancellation without prescribing a database lock. Later design may use an idempotency store, transaction, or workflow engine. A mandated shared service is a constraint with rationale and owner.

### Worked example - decomposition with shared controls

The capability Cancel Eligible Appointment decomposes into retrieve status, evaluate actor authority, evaluate cancellation rule, register cancellation, issue reference, request notification, and expose result. Audit and privacy controls link across these functions. Recovery behavior handles timeout after registration. Product acceptance traces to the use case; atomic functional IDs trace to scenario and test evidence.

## BA-03-C10-T03 - Stable Identifiers and Precise Requirement Statements

### Identity and attributes

A stable identifier survives wording, ordering, and document-layout change. It allows precise review, traceability, version comparison, test linkage, and impact analysis. Do not encode volatile priority, status, or component name into an ID unless the governance scheme explicitly manages the consequence. Retired IDs are not reused.

Useful attributes include title, statement, rationale, source, owner, type, state, applicability, priority, verification method, acceptance link, dependencies, version, and history. Not every field belongs in publication, but the controlled working record retains enough for decisions. A spreadsheet row, repository item, and rendered paragraph should point to the same identity.

### Statement construction

A functional statement identifies the subject, mandatory behavior, trigger or condition, object, and observable result. Use a consistent modal such as shall under the local writing standard. Define terms, quantities, units, timing, and exceptions. Avoid vague adjectives, passive responsibility, weak should, and conjunctions that conceal independent obligations.

Example notation:

```text
FR-APT-021
When an authorized actor submits a cancellation for an Eligible appointment,
the Appointment Service shall record state Cancelled once and return the
same immutable cancellation reference for every retry with the same request key.
```

The statement specifies observable idempotent behavior without dictating storage design. Its linked rule defines Eligible; interface and quality requirements define key format and response time.

### Failure analysis - precise syntax, wrong level

"The React component shall call PostgreSQL using function cancel_v2" is precise but likely a design statement. It prevents alternative architecture and exposes internal implementation to business acceptance. If a justified constraint mandates the stack, record it separately. The functional requirement should describe authorized cancellation, state, response, duplicate behavior, and evidence.

## BA-03-C10-T04 - Actors, Roles, Permissions, and Authorization Behavior

### Authorization model

Authentication establishes evidence about identity; authorization determines whether an actor can perform an action on a resource in context. Specify actor, action, object, scope, condition, delegation, default behavior, revocation, and audit. Role labels alone are insufficient because the same Agent may act only for assigned centers or cases.

Permissions can derive from role, relationship, ownership, purpose, state, risk, location, time, or policy. Separate ability to view, create, change, approve, export, override, and administer. Least privilege and separation of duties constrain access, but operational paths and emergency access need designed behavior.

### Delegation and lifecycle

Representatives, carers, guardians, managers, and service accounts need authority source, scope, period, verification, revocation, and evidence. Do not treat account sharing as delegation. Changes should take effect within a defined period and invalidate sessions or cached permissions where necessary.

Denials should be safe and usable. The system records enough for audit, returns an appropriate result, avoids revealing sensitive existence, and offers recovery or escalation where legitimate. Elevated or break-glass access requires reason, duration, notification, review, and misuse monitoring under specialist control.

### Worked example - authorized representative

An authorized representative may view and cancel appointments within a recorded delegation but cannot alter the person's core identity or contact preference. The service checks active delegation at the action time, records actor and represented person, and notifies through governed channels. Revocation blocks new actions without deleting historical evidence.

## BA-03-C10-T05 - Inputs, Processing, Outputs, and Validations

### IPO specification

For each function identify trigger, input source, required and optional data, preconditions, processing responsibilities, output, state change, side effects, validation, and failure. Validate syntax, semantics, authority, state, cross-field rules, and external facts at the boundary that owns the decision. Client-side validation improves usability but does not replace authoritative validation.

Processing requirements describe necessary transformations and invariants. Sequence matters when effects are irreversible. Specify whether operations are atomic, idempotent, eventually consistent, or compensating. Avoid pretending a distributed operation can roll back every external effect. Expose pending and failed states honestly.

### Outputs and evidence

Outputs include responses, records, events, documents, notifications, reports, and audit. Define content, recipient, timing, format, classification, accessibility, retention, and failure behavior. A success response should represent committed authoritative state, not merely receipt, unless the state is explicitly Accepted or Pending.

Validation messages explain the condition and safe next action without exposing rule internals or personal information. Preserve valid user input where safe. Multiple errors may be returned together for forms, while security-sensitive checks may intentionally limit detail.

### Worked example - cancellation input and output

Input includes appointment identifier, actor context, request key, reason category, and optional comment. Processing checks existence without disclosure, authority, current state, cutoff rule, and duplicate key; then commits cancellation and audit before requesting notification. Output returns reference, authoritative state, effective time, and notification status. A notification failure does not reverse cancellation but creates a monitored retry.

## BA-03-C10-T06 - Data Requirements, Field Rules, and Data Quality

### Semantic data specification

Data requirements define business concepts before physical fields. For each element specify definition, source, owner, type, unit, precision, permitted values, null meaning, derivation, validation, sensitivity, access, lineage, retention, and quality expectation as relevant. Same spelling does not prove same meaning; different labels may represent one concept.

Identify entity identity and relationships. An appointment occurrence differs from a recurring series, booking request, and service episode. Keys, cardinality, temporal validity, and status history affect behavior. A data dictionary, conceptual model, and example records complement prose.

### Field and quality rules

Distinguish missing, unknown, not applicable, withheld, and zero. Define normalization without destroying source evidence. Dates need calendar and timezone; money needs currency and rounding; measurements need unit; names and addresses should not assume one culture. Derived values link to formula and version.

Data quality requirements connect dimension and use: accuracy, completeness, validity, timeliness, uniqueness, consistency, and integrity. Define critical elements, thresholds, detection, quarantine, correction authority, and downstream behavior. A high aggregate completeness score can hide a missing critical field.

### Worked example - appointment time

The service stores scheduled instant, service-center timezone, displayed local time, timezone-rule version where needed, and change history. It handles daylight-saving gaps and overlaps explicitly. A cancellation cutoff rule evaluates against the center's local business calendar, not the viewer's device clock. Tests cover boundary times and historical reproduction.

## BA-03-C10-T07 - Interfaces, APIs, Integrations, and Dependencies

### Interface contract

An interface requirement defines provider, consumer, purpose, operation or event, semantics, schema, validation, authorization, privacy, performance, availability, errors, versioning, observability, support, and ownership. The FRD may link to an OpenAPI, AsyncAPI, file, or message specification, but technical syntax remains connected to business definitions.

Specify direction, initiation, correlation, ordering, duplicate handling, pagination, rate behavior, timeouts, retries, and consistency. A timeout is an unknown result for some commands, not proof of failure. Consumers need a status query or idempotent retry. Events require delivery semantics and handling of late or out-of-order messages.

### Dependency control

External services and suppliers need service commitments, change notice, security, privacy, incident, test environment, support, and exit conditions according to risk. Inventory versions and consumers. Deprecation includes migration evidence and retirement date; leaving abandoned endpoints active increases risk.

OpenAPI provides a standard machine-readable description for HTTP APIs [2], but contract governance still owns semantics and compatibility. Consumer and provider tests can prove example behavior while missing an unauthorized or incorrectly defined business rule.

### Worked example - notification dependency

Appointment sends a notification command with template purpose, recipient token, locale, channel preference, sensitive-content classification, and correlation. Notification returns Accepted or a typed rejection. Delivery events update status. Cancellation remains authoritative if delivery fails; operations sees retry and escalation without receiving unnecessary message content.

## BA-03-C10-T08 - Business Rules, Calculations, and Decision Logic

### Rule control

Rules define structure, derivation, permission, obligation, prohibition, or decision. Each has source authority, owner, applicability, effective dates, version, inputs, outcome, exceptions, and evidence. Keep rules separate from process sequence so they can be reused, tested, and changed consistently.

Decision tables expose combinations, gaps, and overlaps. Define hit policy when more than one row matches. Calculations specify units, order of operations, precision, rounding, boundary inclusion, missing data, and intermediate values. A spreadsheet example can clarify but should not become an uncontrolled source.

### Decision notation

```text
IF appointment.state = Scheduled
AND actor.authorization = Valid
AND current_time <= cancellation_cutoff
THEN eligibility = Eligible
ELSE eligibility = Refer(rule_reason)
```

This simplified logic still needs definitions for current time, cutoff, state, and authorization. OMG Decision Model and Notation provides a formal way to model decisions and requirements [3]. Notation improves precision but does not determine policy authority.

### Worked example - calculation boundary

The cutoff is two center working days before scheduled local time, excluding published closure days. A Friday appointment at 10:00 has different boundary behavior when Monday is a holiday. The FRD links to the controlled calendar source, defines inclusive equality, and returns a refer outcome when calendar data is unavailable rather than guessing.

## BA-03-C10-T09 - Workflows, States, Events, and Transitions

### State model

A state represents a business-significant condition with permitted behavior. Define initial, active, terminal, and exceptional states; events; guards; transitions; actions; invariants; and history. State names are not status labels copied from a screen. The model should prevent impossible combinations and identify which system owns authoritative state.

Workflow describes sequence, responsibility, decisions, handoffs, waiting, parallel work, and compensation. A flowchart can hide state; a state model can hide actor work. Use both where consequence warrants. Events are facts that occurred and should be named in past tense; commands request behavior and may be rejected.

### Transition control

For each transition specify authorized trigger, precondition, guard, resulting state, side effects, evidence, duplicate behavior, and failure. Concurrent events need conflict rules. Long-running work needs pending, timeout, cancellation, escalation, and recovery rather than one synchronous happy path.

```text
Scheduled --CancelAccepted--> Cancelled
Scheduled --ChangeRequested--> ChangePending
ChangePending --ChangeConfirmed--> Rescheduled
ChangePending --ChangeFailed--> Scheduled
```

The first release implements only the cancellation transition; change states remain outside scope and must not appear accidentally through integration.

### Worked example - concurrent cancellation and check-in

Cancellation and center check-in arrive seconds apart. The requirement defines an authoritative ordering rule and returns the resulting state to both actors. If check-in committed first, cancellation is referred; if cancellation committed first, check-in is rejected with safe recovery. Audit preserves timestamps and decision order without relying on client clocks.

## BA-03-C10-T10 - Exceptions, Errors, Alternate Flows, and Recovery

### Failure taxonomy

Distinguish validation failure, authorization denial, business-rule exception, dependency failure, timeout, conflict, duplicate, capacity overload, internal defect, and disaster. Each category has different user message, retry, state, logging, alert, and escalation. "An error occurred" is not sufficient requirement behavior.

Alternate flows are legitimate variants; exceptions require different handling; errors indicate unsuccessful processing; recovery returns the service to a safe condition. Identify minimum guarantee: what remains true even when the goal fails. For a cancellation attempt, the user must be able to determine authoritative state and must not create duplicate cancellation effects.

### Partial completion and reconciliation

Distributed operations can commit locally before an external effect fails. Requirements state source of truth, pending state, retry ownership, compensation, timeout, and reconciliation. Do not tell the user to retry an irreversible operation blindly. Operational queues expose age, reason, owner, and escalation.

Recovery includes user recovery, operational repair, technical restoration, and business reconciliation. Backup restoration is not enough if messages replay or external providers acted during outage. Compare business records and preserve evidence of manual corrections.

### Worked example - notification failure after cancellation

Cancellation commits and returns its reference, but notification times out. The service reports cancellation success and notification pending, queues a retry with the same message key, alerts after threshold, and lets users view status. Operations can change channel under authorization. Recovery never reverses the valid cancellation merely to make the notification transaction appear atomic.

## BA-03-C10-T11 - Links to Non-functional, Security, Privacy, and Compliance Requirements

### Connected quality

Functional behavior operates under quality conditions: performance, capacity, reliability, availability, resilience, usability, accessibility, maintainability, portability, observability, and supportability. Link a function or use case to applicable quality requirements rather than copying a generic list. Each quality statement defines stimulus, environment, subject, response, measure, and evidence.

Security requirements arise from assets, threats, trust boundaries, and risk decisions. Privacy requirements arise from data purpose, authority, minimization, rights, sharing, retention, and lifecycle. Accessibility requirements combine applicable WCAG criteria with end-to-end task evidence [4]. Compliance requirements identify source, interpretation owner, applicability, effective date, and proof.

### Resolve control interaction

Controls can conflict. Detailed logs support investigation but can violate minimization. Short timeouts protect capacity but exclude slow connections. Reauthentication reduces takeover but may block users with inaccessible recovery. Requirements analysis presents options to qualified owners and records the accepted balance; it does not label one control "best practice" and ignore consequence.

Quality and specialist items retain their own stable IDs and trace to relevant behavior, data, interfaces, and tests. A functional requirement should not absorb a security rule in wording that changes independently. Coverage reports show missing links, while semantic review confirms the link is appropriate.

### Worked example - accessible secure cancellation

Cancellation requires recent authentication for high-risk context but offers accessible step-up methods and assisted recovery. Response time excludes neither assistive technology nor slow input. Audit records actor, action, resource, result, and reason without storing free-text health information. The linked controls are reviewed jointly rather than sequentially.

## BA-03-C10-T12 - Test Cases, UAT Criteria, and Implementation Traceability

### Verification relationships

Each requirement identifies a feasible verification method: test, inspection, analysis, demonstration, measurement, or review. Test cases specify environment, configuration, preconditions, data, steps or event, expected result, cleanup, and evidence. Link cases to requirement versions; a passing old test does not verify changed behavior.

Use normal, boundary, negative, alternate, failure, security, accessibility, performance, and recovery evidence according to risk. Decision tables and state transitions support systematic coverage. Expected results come from authorized rules and requirements, not copied implementation output.

### UAT and implementation trace

User acceptance testing evaluates whether the solution supports agreed business and user needs in realistic context. It does not replace system, integration, security, accessibility, or performance testing. UAT criteria identify participants, authority, scenarios, data, environment, evidence, defects, residual risks, and decision.

Implementation trace links requirements to design elements, backlog work, code or configuration changes, tests, releases, operational controls, and measured outcomes as appropriate. The link does not prove correctness, but exposes orphan implementation and missing realization. Generated trace reports should preserve typed relationships and versions.

### Worked example - requirement-to-release chain

FR-APT-021 traces upward to cancellation capability and business outcome; sideways to eligibility rule, appointment data, API contract, authorization, accessibility, and response-time requirements; downward to design decision, implementation change, contract and state tests, recovery rehearsal, UAT scenario, release version, and monitoring. A rule change identifies every affected item for review.

## Integrated FRD construction model

The FRD begins with boundary and actors, decomposes capabilities into governed functions, gives each requirement stable identity, and specifies behavior across authority, input, processing, output, data, interfaces, decisions, state, and failure. Quality and specialist controls constrain behavior. Verification and implementation trace provide evidence and change navigation.

| FRD layer | Principal question | Typical representation |
|---|---|---|
| Context | What subject and external actors are in scope? | Context and trust-boundary model |
| Functional hierarchy | How does behavior support product capability? | Typed decomposition and trace |
| Requirement | What observable obligation applies? | Stable statement and attributes |
| Authorization | Who can do what to which resource? | Permission matrix and scenarios |
| IPO | What enters, changes, leaves, and validates? | Use cases, transformations, examples |
| Data | What does information mean and how is quality controlled? | Data dictionary and conceptual model |
| Interface | How do boundaries exchange governed meaning? | API/event/file contract |
| Decision | Which rules and calculations determine outcomes? | Rule catalogue and decision table |
| State | Which events permit which lifecycle changes? | State and workflow models |
| Failure | How are alternatives, errors, and recovery handled? | Exception catalogue and recovery scenarios |
| Quality | Under what measurable conditions must behavior operate? | Quality scenarios and specialist requirements |
| Evidence | How is behavior verified and traced to release? | Tests, UAT, trace matrix, monitoring |

### End-to-end worked example - one cancellation request

An authorized representative submits cancellation through the web channel. The API validates schema and request identity. Authorization checks active delegation. The appointment service loads authoritative state and applies the effective cutoff rule using center time. It commits one Cancelled transition and audit record, returns an immutable reference, and emits a notification command. Retry returns the same result.

If the notification provider times out, cancellation remains successful and notification becomes Pending. Monitoring and retry manage delivery. Accessible status lets the represented person and authorized agent confirm the result. Contract, decision, state, security, privacy, accessibility, performance, and recovery tests link to their exact requirement versions. The business outcome is evaluated later through completion and capacity measures.

## Advanced functional specification practice

### Model agreement and review

An FRD is a set of coordinated representations. Prose explains obligations and rationale; context diagrams allocate boundaries; use cases organize actor goals; decision tables expose rule combinations; state models govern lifecycle; data definitions govern meaning; interface specifications govern exchange; quality scenarios define measurable conditions; and examples make boundaries concrete. No single representation is complete.

Reviewers should compare them deliberately. If the state model permits Cancelled only from Scheduled but a use case permits cancellation from ChangePending, there is a contradiction. If a decision table treats the cutoff as inclusive while an example treats equality as ineligible, the logic is unresolved. If an API schema permits null appointment time while the requirement says it is mandatory, machine and human specifications disagree.

Establish precedence or, preferably, remove duplication. One authoritative rule can be rendered into a decision table and referenced by scenarios. Where duplication is necessary for reader understanding, define synchronization and check it. Automated validation can detect broken identifiers, duplicate IDs, invalid states, schema mismatch, and missing tests; independent semantic review remains necessary.

### Worked example - detecting cross-model drift

The written rule says representatives can cancel, the permission matrix grants only view, and the UAT scenario succeeds because test accounts carry customer privileges. Each artifact passes local review. Cross-model trace reveals the mismatch. The authority clarifies delegation, the matrix and tests change, and the representative scenario uses a genuine delegated account. The defect was not wording alone; it was inconsistent responsibility across representations.

### Requirements versus design

The distinction between requirement and design is contextual. "The service shall retain an auditable cancellation decision" states a required outcome. "Store it in database table X" is design unless a contractual, regulatory, interoperability, or organizational constraint mandates that store. A selected architecture can create lower-level requirements for a component, but those statements should preserve their source and level.

Premature design can exclude alternatives and transfer assumptions into acceptance. Excessive abstraction can be equally harmful: developers cannot implement "provide a seamless experience." The analyst works with architects, designers, engineers, testers, and specialists to identify the observable boundary and necessary constraints. Design options can be recorded separately and traced to the requirements they satisfy.

When a stakeholder mandates a technology, record who decided, why, applicability, and consequences. The constraint may affect security, accessibility, support, supplier exit, cost, or feasibility. It should not be disguised as an unquestionable business need. If evidence changes, impact analysis can revisit the constraint without rewriting the higher outcome.

### Behavioral precision patterns

Events and conditions reduce ambiguity. "When [event], while [relevant state], the [subject] shall [observable response] within [measure]" is a useful pattern, but not mandatory syntax. State requirements can define invariants. Unwanted behavior can use a prohibition where the writing standard permits it. Ubiquitous requirements apply across a stated boundary and should not be copied into every function.

Quantifiers require a population. "All cancellation requests" should address malformed, unauthorized, duplicate, and out-of-scope requests or use a defined term such as Valid Cancellation Request. Timing requires a start event, stop event, clock, percentile or maximum, load condition, and exclusions. Capacity specifies concurrent or rate conditions and expected degradation.

Definitions are part of precision. Capitalizing Eligible does not govern meaning unless it links to a controlled definition or rule. Avoid synonyms for governed terms. Examples should use representative and boundary values, not only round numbers. Counterexamples are valuable because they show what the requirement excludes.

### Worked example - quality scenario for response time

"The service shall respond quickly" becomes: during published service hours, under up to 200 authenticated cancellation requests per minute, the Appointment API shall return an authoritative success, rejection, or accepted-pending response within two seconds for at least 95 percent of requests measured at the service boundary over each rolling hour. Separate requirements define the remaining percentile, dependency timeout, overload response, and measurement exclusions. The numeric target still needs owner, evidence, and feasibility review.

### Data lifecycle and migration

Functional specifications often focus on create and update while forgetting correction, merge, archival, retention, disposal, and historical reproduction. For each governed entity, define creation authority, source, state, permitted changes, audit, quality, access, retention event, deletion or anonymization, and legal hold where applicable. Derived data and caches need lineage and refresh behavior.

Migration requirements define source and target semantics, mapping, transformation, cleansing authority, duplicates, rejected records, reconciliation, security, privacy, performance, cutover, rollback, and evidence. A row count match does not prove correctness. Compare critical totals, relationships, statuses, samples, and business invariants. Preserve source evidence until authorized reconciliation and retention decisions complete.

Historical cases may remain under old rules or states. The target design must represent applicability rather than converting everything to the latest meaning. If old values are normalized, retain original value and transformation provenance where required. Users and operations need behavior for cases that cannot migrate automatically.

### Worked example - migrating appointment status

The legacy system has Booked, Arrived, DidNotAttend, and Closed; the new service has Scheduled, CheckedIn, Completed, Cancelled, and NoShow. Closed cannot map deterministically. Migration rules use service outcome and event history, quarantine ambiguous cases, and route them to authorized review. Reconciliation checks active counts by center and date, not only total rows. Every transformed case records source ID and mapping version.

### Interface evolution

Interface requirements must anticipate independent change. Semantic version labels help communication but do not replace compatibility analysis. Classify changes to schema, meaning, validation, authorization, timing, errors, ordering, and service levels. A field can remain syntactically compatible while its business definition changes and breaks a consumer's decision.

Define consumer inventory, support windows, change notice, test environments, migration evidence, and retirement authority. A compatibility layer or parallel version may reduce disruption but creates operating cost and data-consistency risk. Time-bound the transition and monitor remaining consumers. Emergency security changes may shorten notice under a governed path.

For event integrations, specify event identity, aggregate or subject identity, occurrence and publication times, schema version, ordering boundary, delivery semantics, replay, retention, privacy classification, and consumer behavior. "Exactly once" is often an unrealistic end-to-end promise; idempotent consumers and reconciliation provide safer requirements.

### Worked example - changing cancellation reason

A provider replaces free-text reason with a governed code list. Existing consumers use text for reports, while one uses it to trigger refunds. The impact analysis separates display, analytics, and decision uses. A transition publishes code and controlled label, maps historical values where defensible, rejects unsafe decision dependence on text, and retains the old field during a defined migration window. Contract tests cover both versions.

### Decision completeness

Decision logic needs more than the happy row. List input domains and examine every permitted combination. Mark impossible, inapplicable, refer, or error outcomes explicitly. Overlap requires a hit policy: first, unique, priority, collect, or another governed rule. A catch-all else can hide missing analysis; use it only when the resulting outcome is authorized and safe.

Calculations identify authoritative input time, currency, precision, rounding mode, unit conversion, intermediate rounding, minimum and maximum, negative values, missing values, and correction. Reproduce examples from the governed formula, not from manually typed expected results. Independent implementation or calculation can verify critical logic.

Rules also have jurisdiction and effective time. A case may use the rule applicable at application, appointment, decision, or service date. Specify the event. Future-dated rule versions can be approved but inactive. Decision records should retain input values, outcome, rule version, and relevant explanation so historical results can be reconstructed.

### Worked example - complete cancellation decision table

The table crosses appointment state, actor authority, cutoff condition, protected-service flag, and center closure. Eligible cancellation requires Scheduled, valid authority, cutoff met, and no protected-service restriction. A closure can extend the cutoff under a policy-owned rule. Missing calendar data yields Refer, not Eligible. Duplicate request bypasses re-evaluation only after matching the original payload and returning its stored result.

### Concurrency and consistency

Requirements for digital systems must address simultaneous action. Two agents may edit a case, a customer may cancel while a center checks in, or a payment callback may arrive after timeout. Specify conflict detection, authoritative ordering, version checks, retry, merge where allowed, and user feedback. Last-write-wins is a design choice with potential data loss, not a neutral default.

Strong consistency, eventual consistency, and cached views produce different user experiences. If a notification or dashboard lags authoritative state, disclose freshness and prevent it from authorizing action. Read-your-writes may be necessary after a user change. The FRD states the business guarantee while architecture selects mechanisms.

Distributed clocks are imperfect. Use server or authority time for decisions, record event occurrence and receipt where relevant, and define tolerance. Client timestamps can support evidence but should not control authorization without validation. Sequence identifiers or versions may be more reliable than wall-clock comparison.

### Worked example - optimistic concurrency

An agent opens appointment version 8. The customer cancels, creating version 9. The agent then submits a note and status change based on version 8. The service rejects the conflicting transition, preserves the note as a draft, returns current state, and prompts review. It does not overwrite cancellation. Audit identifies both attempts and versions.

### Security and abuse behavior

Security requirements include behavior under malicious input and misuse. Analyze object authorization, function authorization, enumeration, excessive resource use, injection, unsafe file or URL handling, server-side request behavior, inventory, secrets, and third-party trust according to the architecture and risk. OWASP's API Security Top 10 provides useful threat prompts but must be translated into system-specific requirements [6].

Specify safe limits and responses. Rate controls identify subject and scope, not merely IP address. Uploaded content has type, size, scanning, storage, access, and disposal rules. External URLs and callbacks need allowlists or validation as appropriate. Secrets do not appear in logs or error messages. Security monitoring itself follows privacy, retention, access, and alert requirements.

Abuse cases complement normal use cases. Ask how an actor could discover another person's appointment, cancel repeatedly, manipulate a cutoff, flood notifications, replay a token, exploit representative authority, or infer sensitive service type. Controls link to threats and verification evidence, while sensitive detail remains access-controlled.

### Accessibility in functional detail

Functional requirements support accessibility by specifying semantics and behavior, not only visual conformance. Inputs have programmatic labels and instructions; errors identify affected fields and recovery; status changes are announced; focus moves predictably; timeouts can be extended where permitted; authentication has accessible alternatives; and documents or notifications remain usable.

The same function across channels should preserve outcome. An assisted agent needs authoritative data and permission; a user relying on keyboard or screen reader needs full control; a person on a slow connection needs recoverable progress. Accessibility testing combines standards-based evaluation, manual inspection, assistive technology, and users. W3C's WCAG 2.2 is a normative source for web content criteria but does not replace product-specific journey analysis [4].

Accessibility requirements must coexist with security and privacy. CAPTCHA, biometric verification, short timeouts, masked information, and session controls can create barriers. Joint review seeks controls that protect all objectives. An alternate route should not impose disproportionate delay, cost, disclosure, or loss of independence.

### Operational readiness and supportability

The FRD identifies operational behavior needed to run the service: health and business monitoring, alerts, queue visibility, support tools, diagnostic correlation, configuration, capacity, continuity, backup, restoration, reconciliation, escalation, and maintenance. Operators should distinguish a dependency failure from invalid user input and know which action is authorized.

Support interfaces require privacy and security. An agent may need status and reason category without access to full personal evidence. Impersonation should not be the default support method. Manual corrections need authority, validation, audit, notification, and reversal. Repeated manual repair should trigger root-cause review.

Operational documentation, training, staffing, and supplier arrangements are transition requirements linked to functional and quality behavior. A runbook cannot compensate for missing system recovery, but it can specify the coordinated response. Rehearsal produces evidence before high-consequence release.

### Worked example - recovery rehearsal exposes a gap

The team restores the appointment database from backup and sees all rows healthy. Reconciliation shows that notification events emitted after the backup point are missing while the provider delivered them. The FRD adds event replay boundaries, delivery-status import, duplicate-safe reconciliation, and a business total check. Infrastructure recovery alone had not restored consistent service state.

### Traceability depth and proportionality

Traceability should answer decisions, not maximize links. High-consequence rules may trace from legal or policy source through business requirement, product capability, functional requirement, design, code, test, release, and operational evidence. A low-risk display preference may need fewer layers. Define mandatory link types by item class and lifecycle stage.

Coverage checks find requirements without verification, tests without governed requirements, business outcomes without realization, and implementation without authorization. Suspect links receive review; a 100 percent matrix can still be semantically wrong. Orphan implementation may be necessary technical work, accidental scope, or gold plating, and each requires a different response.

Change analysis traverses the network in both directions and then supplements it with expert review. A modified data definition can affect rules and reports not explicitly linked. A changed security constraint can alter user experience and support. Trace tooling accelerates navigation but cannot replace knowledge of context.

### Review and baseline of an FRD

Functional review involves business and product owners for intent, domain experts for rules and data, architecture and engineering for feasibility, testers for verifiability, operations for support and recovery, and specialists for security, privacy, accessibility, legal, safety, and compliance. Users or representatives examine relevant behavior and recovery. Review the same controlled version.

Comments name item, concern, consequence, evidence, owner, disposition, rationale, and changed version. Walkthroughs and scenario simulations reveal interaction defects; model reviews reveal contradiction; prototype and contract tests reveal interpretation. Approval identifies scope and conditions. Unresolved accepted risk remains visible.

The baseline can be layered. Stable interface and policy behavior may be approved while selected low-risk content remains iterative. Define what is controlled and what can change under delegated authority. Subsequent product or technical learning that alters approved intent triggers impact analysis rather than silent document editing.

### Final integrity test

Choose any requirement and ask: who or what must behave, under which trigger and state, with which data and authority, according to which rule version, producing what observable result, under which quality conditions, and how will failure and recovery work? Then ask why the requirement exists, who owns it, how it is verified, and where it is realized. Missing answers identify the next review.

Choose any delivered behavior and trace backward. If it has no authorized requirement, determine whether it is necessary design, enabling work, or uncontrolled scope. Choose any business outcome and trace forward. If it has no behavior and evidence, the specification is incomplete. This bidirectional test keeps the FRD connected to value without turning it into a duplicate BRD.

### Tailoring the functional specification

Tailoring follows consequence and coordination need. A small, reversible internal workflow may use linked stories, examples, a state sketch, data definitions, and quality criteria. A regulated decision service with several suppliers may require a formal FRD, interface specifications, rule models, security and privacy requirements, trace matrices, signatures, and configuration audits. The same quality principles apply even when packaging differs.

Remove template sections that do not support a decision, but do not remove the underlying responsibility. If there is no external API, the interface section can state its boundary and rationale. If a function handles no personal data, privacy review can record that conclusion and scope. Marking an item not applicable with evidence is stronger than generic prose and safer than silent omission.

Keep normative requirements distinct from explanatory information. Words such as shall, must, may, and should need defined local meanings. Examples normally clarify but do not override a normative rule. Rationale explains why and supports change analysis. Notes and design proposals remain labeled. Without these distinctions, a reviewer cannot tell which sentence is approved behavior.

### Specification quality metrics

Useful measures include missing sources, invalid identifiers, unresolved TBDs, requirements without verification, conflicting rules, uncovered states, unhandled interface errors, broken traces, review age, and volatility after baseline. Interpret counts with risk. One unresolved authorization rule can matter more than fifty missing low-risk links. A low defect count can indicate quality or superficial review.

TBD and TBC items need owner, question, consequence, and resolution date. A placeholder inside an approved baseline is an accepted condition only when authority and impact are explicit. Do not convert unknowns into ambiguous defaults to make a completeness report green. A readiness gate can permit bounded unknowns while preventing release of affected behavior.

Review effectiveness can be sampled through escaped defects and comment quality. If testing repeatedly finds missing exceptions, improve scenario and state review. If operations finds reconciliation gaps, include recovery rehearsal earlier. The objective is learning, not rewarding authors for low comment counts.

### Handover and maintainability

An FRD remains useful after the initial team changes. It identifies owners, current versions, retrieval, definitions, decision sources, model notation, test evidence, and change process. New maintainers should not need private chat history to understand why an exception exists or which rule is authoritative.

Handover includes known limitations, open decisions, deferred scope, temporary controls, support obligations, and retirement plans. Demonstrate navigation from outcome to requirement to implementation and from incident back to affected rules and tests. A document walkthrough without repository access and trace verification is only orientation.

The learner-facing publication can remove internal IDs, statuses, and audit tables, but production work requires them. Code-free presentation and coded control are different views over one source. This maintains readability without sacrificing the ability to identify and correct a specific module, chapter, topic, requirement, or trace link.

Ready for independent review means these relationships and boundaries are explicit enough to challenge. It does not mean the author has accepted technical feasibility, policy correctness, risk, accessibility, or business value. Those judgments remain with qualified reviewers and authorized owners examining the controlled version.

Every accepted correction creates a new governed revision with preserved history and renewed impact review where necessary.

## Common misconceptions

- An FRD is not a user-interface design or database schema.
- Precision does not justify unnecessary implementation constraints.
- A role list does not specify authorization behavior.
- Client validation does not replace authoritative validation.
- A machine-readable API schema does not govern business semantics by itself.
- A happy-path workflow is not a complete state model.
- Logging is not equivalent to safe recovery or reconciliation.
- UAT does not replace specialist and technical verification.
- Trace links show relationships but still require semantic review.

## Ethics, limitations, and professional boundaries

Detailed specifications can scale harm efficiently when their source, population, or controls are wrong. Review authorization, decision rules, data use, error behavior, accessibility, automation boundaries, and recovery with affected users and qualified specialists. Avoid publishing secrets, personal evidence, exploitable control detail, or proprietary interfaces beyond authorized access.

The analyst does not determine legal interpretation, accept security risk, certify accessibility, or approve architecture unless formally assigned and competent. The analyst makes the behavior and decision boundaries explicit, routes specialist questions, and preserves evidence. Generative tools may assist structure but cannot invent policy, interfaces, results, or approval.

## Purpose

This chapter develops the controlled theory for waterfall and hybrid within Agile, Scrum, Kanban, and Waterfall. It defines the approved concepts, shows how an analyst applies them to business evidence and decisions, and establishes the controls needed to avoid false confidence.

The chapter contains Phase 1 theory only. It does not contain MCQs, exercises, assignments, implementation labs, guided projects, case studies, capstone work, collation, or release artifacts.

## Learning outcomes

After completing this chapter, the learner should be able to define every controlled concept, apply it to an appropriate analytical or delivery question, identify evidence and governance needs, recognize common failure modes, and explain the limits of the resulting conclusion.

## BA-07-C05-T01 - stages

### Concept

A predictive or Waterfall lifecycle organizes work into planned stages such as conception, requirements, design, build, verification, deployment, and operation. The analyst must connect this meaning to a defined business question, population, decision, and accountable owner.

### Analysis and application

Apply stages by recording the purpose, input evidence, assumptions, calculation or decision rule, output, and consumer. Within Waterfall and Hybrid, compare the result with an independent source or expected condition and retain enough detail for another reviewer to reproduce the reasoning.

### Quality checks and limitations

Define deliverables and exit evidence while allowing necessary feedback; real work rarely becomes risk-free merely because stages are sequential. Document exceptions, access or privacy implications, material uncertainty, and the escalation path before the output is relied upon.

## Integrated waterfall and hybrid model

Begin with the decision and its accountable owner. Define the population, scope, terminology, evidence, and time boundary before selecting a technique. Preserve source and transformation provenance, test assumptions and exceptions, reconcile the result, and communicate uncertainty beside the conclusion. A technically correct artifact is incomplete until its business meaning, limits, access, maintenance, and response path are controlled.

## Common misconceptions

- A polished output does not prove that the underlying definition, population, or evidence is correct.
- A named tool or framework does not remove the need for analytical judgment and accountable decisions.
- More detail, automation, precision, or ceremony is not automatically more reliable.
- A result that worked once is not governed until ownership, refresh or review, change, and failure handling are defined.
- Correlation, sequence, or consensus alone does not establish causality or correctness.

## Ethics, controls, and professional boundaries

Analytical and delivery choices can redistribute access, attention, workload, delay, monitoring, and risk. Use only necessary data, protect confidential information, test for systematically excluded or harmed groups, make uncertainty visible, and preserve dissent and evidence. Business analysts structure decisions but do not replace legal, privacy, security, accessibility, statistical, safety, compliance, or other qualified specialists.

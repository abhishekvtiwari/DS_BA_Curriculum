## Purpose

This chapter develops the controlled theory for data literacy within Data, SQL, and Analytics for Analysts. It defines the approved concepts, shows how an analyst applies them to business evidence and decisions, and establishes the controls needed to avoid false confidence.

The chapter contains Phase 1 theory only. It does not contain MCQs, exercises, assignments, implementation labs, guided projects, case studies, capstone work, collation, or release artifacts.

## Learning outcomes

After completing this chapter, the learner should be able to define every controlled concept, apply it to an appropriate analytical or delivery question, identify evidence and governance needs, recognize common failure modes, and explain the limits of the resulting conclusion.

## BA-05-C01-T01 - tables

### Concept

A table represents observations as rows and attributes as columns under a defined schema. Its meaning depends on what one row represents, not merely on column labels. The analyst must connect this meaning to a defined business question, population, decision, and accountable owner.

### Analysis and application

Apply tables by recording the purpose, input evidence, assumptions, calculation or decision rule, output, and consumer. Within Data Literacy, compare the result with an independent source or expected condition and retain enough detail for another reviewer to reproduce the reasoning.

### Quality checks and limitations

Do not combine tables until row grain, key behavior, units, time basis, and permissible nulls are understood. Document exceptions, access or privacy implications, material uncertainty, and the escalation path before the output is relied upon.

## BA-05-C01-T02 - grain

### Concept

Grain is the precise real-world entity or event represented by one row. It determines which comparisons, joins, and aggregations are valid. The analyst must connect this meaning to a defined business question, population, decision, and accountable owner.

### Analysis and application

Apply grain by recording the purpose, input evidence, assumptions, calculation or decision rule, output, and consumer. Within Data Literacy, compare the result with an independent source or expected condition and retain enough detail for another reviewer to reproduce the reasoning.

### Quality checks and limitations

## BA-05-C01-T03 - keys

### Concept

A key is one field or a combination of fields used to identify a row or connect related records. Candidate, primary, natural, and surrogate keys serve different purposes. The analyst must connect this meaning to a defined business question, population, decision, and accountable owner.

### Analysis and application

Apply keys by recording the purpose, input evidence, assumptions, calculation or decision rule, output, and consumer. Within Data Literacy, compare the result with an independent source or expected condition and retain enough detail for another reviewer to reproduce the reasoning.

### Quality checks and limitations

## BA-05-C01-T05 - missingness

### Concept

Missingness means an expected value is absent or represented by a sentinel. Blank, unknown, not applicable, not yet known, and withheld are different states. The analyst must connect this meaning to a defined business question, population, decision, and accountable owner.

### Analysis and application

Apply missingness by recording the purpose, input evidence, assumptions, calculation or decision rule, output, and consumer. Within Data Literacy, compare the result with an independent source or expected condition and retain enough detail for another reviewer to reproduce the reasoning.

### Quality checks and limitations

Measure missingness by relevant segment and investigate its generation process before deleting rows, filling values, or treating absence as zero. Document exceptions, access or privacy implications, material uncertainty, and the escalation path before the output is relied upon.

## BA-05-C01-T06 - duplicates

### Concept

Duplicates are records that repeat the same business entity, event, or fact beyond the intended grain. Exact row equality is only one form of duplication. The analyst must connect this meaning to a defined business question, population, decision, and accountable owner.

### Analysis and application

Apply duplicates by recording the purpose, input evidence, assumptions, calculation or decision rule, output, and consumer. Within Data Literacy, compare the result with an independent source or expected condition and retain enough detail for another reviewer to reproduce the reasoning.

### Quality checks and limitations

## Integrated data literacy model

Begin with the decision and its accountable owner. Define the population, scope, terminology, evidence, and time boundary before selecting a technique. Preserve source and transformation provenance, test assumptions and exceptions, reconcile the result, and communicate uncertainty beside the conclusion. A technically correct artifact is incomplete until its business meaning, limits, access, maintenance, and response path are controlled.

## Common misconceptions

- A polished output does not prove that the underlying definition, population, or evidence is correct.
- A named tool or framework does not remove the need for analytical judgment and accountable decisions.
- More detail, automation, precision, or ceremony is not automatically more reliable.
- A result that worked once is not governed until ownership, refresh or review, change, and failure handling are defined.
- Correlation, sequence, or consensus alone does not establish causality or correctness.

## Ethics, controls, and professional boundaries

Analytical and delivery choices can redistribute access, attention, workload, delay, monitoring, and risk. Use only necessary data, protect confidential information, test for systematically excluded or harmed groups, make uncertainty visible, and preserve dissent and evidence. Business analysts structure decisions but do not replace legal, privacy, security, accessibility, statistical, safety, compliance, or other qualified specialists.

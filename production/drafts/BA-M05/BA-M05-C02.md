## Purpose

This chapter develops the controlled theory for spreadsheet analysis within Data, SQL, and Analytics for Analysts. It defines the approved concepts, shows how an analyst applies them to business evidence and decisions, and establishes the controls needed to avoid false confidence.

## BA-05-C02-T01 - formulas

### Concept

Spreadsheet formulas express calculations through cell references, operators, and functions. Relative, absolute, and mixed references change how logic behaves when copied. The analyst must connect this meaning to a defined business question, population, decision, and accountable owner.

### Analysis and application

Apply formulas by recording the purpose, input evidence, assumptions, calculation or decision rule, output, and consumer. Within Spreadsheet Analysis, compare the result with an independent source or expected condition and retain enough detail for another reviewer to reproduce the reasoning.

### Quality checks and limitations

Prefer readable formulas, controlled inputs, explicit error handling, and independent spot checks; hidden constants and copied-reference drift are common defects. Document exceptions, access or privacy implications, material uncertainty, and the escalation path before the output is relied upon.

## Integrated spreadsheet analysis model

Begin with the decision and its accountable owner. Define the population, scope, terminology, evidence, and time boundary before selecting a technique. Preserve source and transformation provenance, test assumptions and exceptions, reconcile the result, and communicate uncertainty beside the conclusion. A technically correct artifact is incomplete until its business meaning, limits, access, maintenance, and response path are controlled.

## Common misconceptions

- A polished output does not prove that the underlying definition, population, or evidence is correct.
- A named tool or framework does not remove the need for analytical judgment and accountable decisions.
- More detail, automation, precision, or ceremony is not automatically more reliable.
- A result that worked once is not governed until ownership, refresh or review, change, and failure handling are defined.
- Correlation, sequence, or consensus alone does not establish causality or correctness.

## Ethics, controls, and professional boundaries

Analytical and delivery choices can redistribute access, attention, workload, delay, monitoring, and risk. Use only necessary data, protect confidential information, test for systematically excluded or harmed groups, make uncertainty visible, and preserve dissent and evidence. Business analysts structure decisions but do not replace legal, privacy, security, accessibility, statistical, safety, compliance, or other qualified specialists.

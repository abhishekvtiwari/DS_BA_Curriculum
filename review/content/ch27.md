# Ch 27 — Capstone: Your Analyst Portfolio
Verdict: One of the best chapters in Part II: a single real question carried from SQL to memo, a headline that is true and wrong, the segment split that breaks it, a threshold-sensitivity what-if, and an unusually honest treatment of selective reporting and portfolios. Numbers are internally consistent to the rounding (I recomputed ~25). The problems: the chapter says it "teaches almost nothing new" and that "every output… was produced by running the query or the script shown", but it introduces NTILE (first taught in Ch 28) and prints at least six results whose code is never shown (duplicate-merge re-run, t-test CI and p, Pearson r, margin by segment, the reconciliation, answer 5's composition table); it says the analysis uses the merged-duplicates data but every later number is the as-loaded data; about ten section cross-references point to the wrong section; and the analysis never tests discount depth inside Retail, which is the segment Vikram asked about.
Size: ~1,077 extracted lines, 20 pages (pp. 465–484), Time needed stated: "8–12 hours for the project, spread over a week. The reading is about an hour."; plausible? Reading: no — with four SQL queries, two functions and five what-ifs to run and understand, 2–3 hours. Project: no — it asks for three portfolio projects (one with the full six-stage arc, a dashboard, a memo, FINDINGS.md, a README each, recorded pitches); "Budget a week, not an evening" is right, 8–12 hours is not. Say 20–30 hours over one to two weeks, or scope the chapter's project to the deep project only (8–12 h) and make the other two a follow-on.

## What works (keep)
- The three portfolio-question tests (§27.1) and the written mini-requirement ("claim to test, unit of analysis, period, measures, what would change the recommendation, out of scope") — Ch 25's discipline applied to analysis.
- §27.4's reveal: "Wholesale has no 'under 5%' row at all" → "The headline said nothing about discounting", followed by the within-Wholesale comparison and the careful reading of CI vs a "significant" r = −0.081 at n = 662. Figure 27.2 ("Every bar is true") is an excellent teaching figure.
- §27.5's parameters-as-arguments design and the "measured what-if" on `band_cut` (5 → 3 shrinks the order gap by 36%), plus answer 5's insight that raising the cut is "a dial on segment purity wearing the label of discount depth".
- §27.8's five selective-reporting moves, the four cheap fixes, and §27.9's ninety-second table — practical, specific, and rarely taught.

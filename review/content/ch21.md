# Ch 21 — Descriptive Statistics & Probability
Verdict: Accurate and well-motivated. The Riverstone delivery data runs through the whole chapter, the business framing (promises written as percentiles) is excellent, and almost every printed number I could recompute checks out. For a first-time reader it has three serious gaps. First, it promises that "every formula here is explained twice, once in symbols and once in English", but no formula for the mean, variance, SD, z-score, binomial, Poisson or Bayes' denominator is ever written in symbols and explained symbol by symbol. Second, there is no by-hand step: every idea goes straight to a 45,040-row pandas/scipy call. Third, scipy's `cdf`/`pmf`/`ppf` are used without being explained. There is also one wrong number in the prose ("10 late … happens about 12% of the time" when the output says 17.9%) and a broken cross-reference ("Chapter 24, Forecasting").
Size: ~725 lines, 16 book pages (362–377), 17 code blocks, 29 exercises + timed challenge. Time needed stated: 15–18 hours over two weeks. Plausible? Yes in total, but it is too much for one "chapter's worth" of new ideas: descriptive statistics, four distributions, probability rules, Bayes and the CLT, with no checkpoint between them. Adding the by-hand and spreadsheet steps required below adds about 3 hours, so restate it as 18–21 hours in two parts (see 21.38).

## What works (keep)
- The service-level framing in §21.3 and "In the real world: the seven-day promise": median, p95 and share-inside-promise per branch, with the decision (branch promises at p90) shown. This is exactly how a senior analyst works. Keep the table and the three "obvious in a minute" points.
- The Bayes crate example (§21.7) ends in natural frequencies ("out of 10,000 crates: 180 true flags, 490 false flags"). All figures are correct (P(flag) = 0.067, P(defect | flag) = 0.2687).
- The sampling simulation (§21.8), in which the reader sees the SE shrink with √n, and the standard-error calculation (₹1,288.42, ±₹2,525), both exact.
- "Then say it in sentences, because a table is not a finding" (§21.9). The model paragraph and the "Common mistakes" table are strong and reusable.

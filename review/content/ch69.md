# Ch 69 — The Extra-Points Method
Verdict: The strongest idea in Part VIII, and well written. "Answer first, then depth", the five-dimension rubric, and the twelve named moves give a reader habits they can practise right away. For a first-time reader the main problems are: (1) the 20 practice questions have no answers anywhere (the Appendix G note is a drafting leftover, and the answers it promises don't exist), so the chapter's whole drill can't be self-checked; (2) the chapter promises "a before/after for each move" but only Moves 1 and 5 get one, and the move tags used in §69.4 and in the banks ([+Structure], [+Depth], [Maintenance]…) don't match the twelve move names, so the "same tags everywhere" promise fails; (3) "Before you start: nothing technical" isn't true of the exercises, which need Chapters 12–49 and have no "Learn it in" pointers or difficulty/role labels; (4) the rubric is presented as how interviewers "actually score", with no source, and §69.4's revenue tree has a formula slip.
Size: 408 extracted lines, about 11 pages (pp. 26–36). No code; one formula (month-over-month growth) and one metric tree. 12 moves, 1 fully worked question, 20 exercises (no answers), 1 project. Time needed stated: "3–4 hours to read and drill once". Plausible? Partly. Reading is ~1 hour. "Drill once" means answering 20 questions at three tiers each (60 answers) plus the project; that's 5–7 hours. Restate as "1 hour to read; 4–6 hours to drill all twenty exercises (or pick the five for your role)".

## What works (keep)
- §69.2 weak vs strong shape on the duplicates question: same content, different order. It's the single most useful paragraph in Part VIII.
- §69.4's revenue-drop answer, with each sentence labelled by the move it earns, shows the method working as a whole; the "Likely follow-ups" and "Red flags" lines model what the banks do.
- §69.6 "What not to do" (over-clarifying, caveat overload, vague business talk) stops the method being over-applied; "seasoning, not the whole meal" is memorable.
- §69.5 maps moves to rounds and says when *not* to use the full treatment, which beginners need.

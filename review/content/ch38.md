# Ch 38 — Unsupervised Learning
Verdict: A thoughtful, unusually honest chapter. It says plainly that Riverstone's customers form a continuum, not natural types. It makes stability and an external check the real tests. The basket arithmetic, the six-account k-means and every cross-table I could recompute are right. For a first-time reader, only k-means and market-basket support/confidence/lift get the full treatment (idea → by hand → code). Hierarchical clustering, DBSCAN, the silhouette, the adjusted Rand index, isolation forests and Apriori have no hand example. The dendrogram and the three projection pictures appear without the code that draws them, and PCA loadings are promised but never shown. Correctness problems: a wrong claim about which raw columns have the largest spread (§38.0 and Answer 5); "drums almost always come with a tap fitting" against the chapter's own 29.3% without one; the story quoting cluster 1's churn as 2.6% (that's cluster 0; cluster 1's is 3.5%); a name ("Growing regulars") no measured number supports; and three wrong cross-references.
Size: ~1,260 lines, 31 book pages (133–163), 15 code blocks in the body plus 9 in the answers, 16 exercises. Time needed stated: 8–12 hours over one to two weeks; plausible? Roughly yes for the current text, which covers eight techniques. With the by-hand steps below, restate it as 11–14 hours (38.38).

## What works (keep)
- The honesty thread. Silhouette 0.277 → "a convenient division of a continuous customer base, not a discovery of natural types"; DBSCAN's "honest answer is that there are no density-separated groups"; Answer 12 ("clustering finds groups whether or not the data contains any").
- k-means by hand on six accounts, then the same in NumPy, then scikit-learn giving identical centroids. That is the book's ideal sequence, and every number is right (37.25 → 15.98; 1.313 + 14.67).
- Market basket: support, confidence and lift by hand (4,170 / 6,500 / 2,785 → lift 3.49), then the same in pandas, then Apriori. Plus the confidence trap (lift 0.74) and a concrete "turn rules into money" query.
- Profiling → naming → "What to do" table, and the external churn check (cluster-only AUC 0.759 vs 0.80–0.85 supervised) with the right conclusion: "when you have a target, use it".

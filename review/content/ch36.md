# Ch 36 — The Machine Learning Workflow & Feature Engineering
Verdict: Senior-level content that teaches the right habits: a written prediction moment, censoring, a time split, leakage measured column by column, target encoding done wrong and then right, and baselines before bragging. Every number I could reconcile holds together (row counts, wins by split and source, base-rate log losses, missingness shares). For a first-time reader it fails at the most important point: this is the reader's first scikit-learn chapter, and scikit-learn arrives as a 40-line nested `make_model` in §36.4. Ten classes appear at once. `fit`, `predict`/`predict_proba` and `[:, 1]` are never introduced, and each piece is explained only 2–4 sections later. `train_test_split`, `stratify` and `test_size` appear only at §36.9, unexplained. There are also wrong cross-references (Ch 52 and Ch 47 for deployment and pipelines), a false claim about HistGradientBoosting and sparse input, and no install step.
Size: 1,566 lines, 39 book pages (48–86), 17 code blocks plus 9 in the answers, 16 exercises, a 10-step project. Time needed stated: 8–12 hours over one to two weeks, including the project; plausible? No. The chapter introduces about 25 scikit-learn names and a 12,294-row dataset, and the project alone (10 steps, with a leakage audit of every column) is 4–6 hours. With the scikit-learn on-ramp required below (36.1–36.2), restate it as 14–18 hours over two weeks, in three sittings (36.28).

## What works (keep)
- The framing table (§36.1: decision, unit, target, prediction moment, allowed information, success measure) and "every feature must pass one test". This is the most valuable thing in Part IV for a working analyst.
- The leakage section measures each leak (has_quote +0.155 AUC, days_in_pipeline +0.052, first_response_hours +0.008). It names the small plausible leak as the dangerous one and adds the `logged_at` vs `activity_at` clock check. The target-encoding contamination demo (0.845 → 0.823 once the encoder is inside the pipeline) and the CV proof (0.82 vs 0.78) make an abstract warning concrete.
- The isolated OneHotEncoder demo in §36.6 (fit on training rows, an unseen "Newspaper ad" → all zeros, `get_feature_names_out`, sparse → `.toarray()`) is exactly the one-thing-at-a-time teaching the rest of the scikit-learn material needs.
- Baselines (§36.9: base rate, three-source rule, simple model) with a one-time test, and the honest reading "around 0.8, clearly better than the current rule". The "Vikram's 98% model" story mirrors the chapter's method well.

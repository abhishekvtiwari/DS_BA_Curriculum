# Ch 43 — A First Look at Deep Learning
Verdict: The argument is honest and well built: a neuron is logistic regression, XOR shows why depth matters, a forward pass matches PyTorch, and the tabular comparison is fair. For a first-time reader, though, this is the densest code jump in Part IV. PyTorch is never installed or introduced (the `pip install torch` line sits in the end-of-chapter Tools box), and the first PyTorch cell uses about nine new objects with no line-by-line explanation (`nn.Sequential`, `nn.Linear`, `SGD`, `BCELoss`, `zero_grad`, `backward`, `step`…). The chapter promises a by-hand backward pass ("This chapter has you do it by hand once") but never works the chain rule. It only compares autograd with a numerical nudge, and it explains the nudge's error wrongly. The transfer-learning comparison is not controlled: transfer and scratch models train on different random samples. Exercise 5's answer claims something that isn't true.
Size: ~1,170 lines / 29 pages (287–315). Time needed stated: 8–12 hours over one to two weeks; plausible? Roughly yes for the current text, with 16 exercises and a 7-step project. Once the missing PyTorch primer, the hand chain rule, and line-by-line explanations are added, 12–15 hours is more honest. The "runs in minutes on a CPU" claim is plausible (small data, full-batch training).

## What works (keep)
- §43.1–43.3 build from what the reader already knows: a neuron is Ch 37's logistic regression (same `z`, same sigmoid), Sharma Hardware's vector comes from Ch 35, and `W1 @ x` reuses Ch 35's matrix product. The by-hand forward pass matching PyTorch to 1e-9 is exactly the "worked by hand, then in the tool" principle. I recomputed it and it holds.
- The XOR pair of runs (single neuron stuck at ln 2 = 0.6931; 4-unit hidden layer solves it), followed by Exercise 6 (2 hidden units work, 1 doesn't), makes "why depth" concrete and correct.
- §43.5's honest comparison on the same split and pipeline as Ch 37 (logistic 0.764 / net 0.773 / tuned boosting 0.788, identical to Ch 37's numbers). The vendor story (91% accuracy vs the 90.3% "no churn" baseline) teaches the book's baseline discipline well.
- The transfer-learning section reports a mixed result honestly instead of a flattering one, and explains why (small, narrow base task).

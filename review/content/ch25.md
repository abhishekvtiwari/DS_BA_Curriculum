# Ch 25 — The Business Analyst Track
Verdict: A strong, well-judged chapter: the four verbs, the four data products, the data-specific NFRs, the reconciliation criterion and the UAT story are exactly what a first-time reader needs, and it needs no tools. Its problems are consistency, not teaching: it rewrites Chapter 3's invoicing facts (Ch 3 says the ERP already raises the invoice automatically on Shipped; Ch 25 builds a BR, a gap analysis and a requirement around a manual 1–2 day delay), its requirement IDs collide (FR-11/NFR-04 used twice, figure and text disagree, BR-06 cited but never written), and it misquotes Chapter 58's 88% figure and builds an exercise on the misquote. A few terms (QA, UAT, features, "held back from training", "warehouse" meaning data warehouse) are used before they are defined.
Size: ~968 extracted lines, 18 pages (pp. 426–443), Time needed stated: 5–7 hours; plausible? Roughly — reading ~1.5 h, 15 exercises ~2 h, the 7-step project realistically 3–4 h for a first-timer (swimlane + 3 gap rows + spec + 2 stories + UAT plan). Say 6–8 hours, or split into two sittings (§25.1–25.5, then §25.6–25.12 + project).

## What works (keep)
- The four-role table plus the four verbs (elicit, analyze, specify, validate) and "the part of every data job that happens before the code" — clear, memorable, and honest about why DA/DS/DE readers need it.
- §25.5's data-specific non-functional requirements table (freshness, grain, completeness, reconciliation, timeliness, history, access, volume) and §25.8's "fourth kind" of acceptance criterion (reconciliation, AC-5). This is the chapter's most original and most valuable content.
- §25.10 "QA verifies the system works as specified; UAT verifies the specification was right", with the two-half data UAT plan — and the real-world story (Ayesha, "UAT failed in eleven minutes") that makes it stick by tying back to §25.3's missing question.
- The swimlane of order 5001 re-using Chapter 3's dates, with "every handoff is where a data quality problem is born" linking process work to the columns the reader cleaned in Ch 14.

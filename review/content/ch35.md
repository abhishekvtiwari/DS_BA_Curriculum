# Ch 35 — The Math Under the Models
Verdict: One of the best-built chapters in the book. Almost every idea follows plain idea → by hand with small numbers → NumPy, and I reproduced every recomputable number exactly, including the gradient-descent table, the learning-rate runs, the likelihoods, the entropies, log loss and the hand PCA. For a first-time reader, five gaps remain. (1) scikit-learn is imported in §35.9–35.10, but no chapter installs it. (2) Logarithms (ln, log₂, e) are never taught anywhere in the book, yet §35.8–35.9 and Ch 36 depend on them. (3) Three key steps are stated without being built: the derivative formula ("Calculus gives…"), the closed-form best w, and the eigenvector equation. (4) Matrix × matrix is used but never taught. (5) The "91% lead score" story (30 leads, 20% win rate, a 0.50 benchmark, "fewer leads than a month") contradicts Ch 36's full CRM of about 400 enquiries a month at 7.7%.
Size: 1,641 lines, 43 book pages (5–47), 17 code blocks plus 9 in the answers, 16 exercises. Time needed stated: 10–14 hours over two weeks; plausible? Only just, for the current text. The chapter holds six subject areas (linear algebra, calculus/optimisation, distributions, likelihood, information theory, PCA). The additions below (logs box, derivative build-up, matrix × matrix, eigen check, install cell) add about 3 hours. Restate it as 14–17 hours over two to three weeks, with named sittings (35.25).

## What works (keep)
- The gradient-descent sequence (§35.4–35.6) is exemplary teaching. A 4-row dataset, the loss by hand, the derivative measured by nudging and then checked against the formula (−584.93 vs −585), three steps by hand matched to the code digit for digit, then the narrow-valley diagnosis and the centering fix. All numbers reproduce exactly (40,511.25 → 4,115.66 → 647.26 → 316.36; 1,000 steps w = 27.12, b = −25.00; centered 28.51/−37.21; divergence boundary 0.01447 = 2 ÷ 138.26).
- The "Reconcile three ideas" box in §35.9 shows the base-rate log loss (0.5004 nats) = entropy (0.7219 bits) = −log-likelihood/30. It turns three abstractions into one number the reader has computed three ways.
- Unscaled vs standardized PCA (§35.10), with "99.8% that means nothing", teaches the most common real PCA mistake with real data. The segment table that "sorts itself" is a strong payoff.
- The "which math sits under which model" map (§35.11) and the Common-mistakes table (each with a symptom the reader will actually see, e.g. the matmul ValueError text) are excellent reference material.

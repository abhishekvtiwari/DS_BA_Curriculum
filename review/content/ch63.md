# Ch 63 — Automation Architecture & Governance
Verdict: A practical, humane chapter. The electrician's-audit analogy, the six-layer reference architecture ("a macro compresses all six layers"), risk as a separate axis, and the audit story are exactly right for a reader moving from builder to architect. The weaknesses are in the numbers. The inventory counts contradict each other (4 duplicated macros vs "two branches"; 24 automations vs "40+" and "eight or nine"). The ROI table misquotes Chapter 19 and Chapter 58, mixes units and loaded costs, and is laid out as a wrapping text block. The chapter repeats Part VII's wrong references (semantic layer "Ch 23", warehouse "Postgres"). "iPaaS" and "polling" appear undefined, and PACELC is stretched to cover scheduled vs event-driven design.
Size: ~880 lines / 21 pages; Time needed stated: 12–14 hours over a week and a half; plausible? Yes. About 3 h of reading; the project (score 24 automations, classify, design services, write a handover note and runbook, controls, a CoE charter) is 8–10 h.

## What works (keep)
- §63.4's reference architecture, with a macro mapped onto every layer (Workbooks.Open = ingestion, the Master sheet = warehouse). It lets a reader govern any automation, from VBA to Dagster.
- §63.2 puts risk on its own axis and uses Chapter 58's real result, where straight-through loses despite better labour numbers. Strong continuity.
- §63.9 "controls scale with risk, not size", with the five-line financial script vs thousand-line read-only report contrast.
- The audit story treats findings as gaps, not blame, and makes the inventory a recurring habit.

# Ch 31 — Causal Inference Without Experiments
Verdict: The best argued chapter in Part III. The counterfactual framing, the "mark the homework against a known true effect" design, the four-sentence write-up, the evidence ranking and the honest answers (exercises 9, 11, 13) are excellent, and almost every printed number checks out: the DiD table, the regression coefficients, the bandwidth table, the bunching counts and the sensitivity share all reconcile. For a first-time reader it is a large jump. Six new mechanisms arrive as finished code with no by-hand or spreadsheet step: logarithms, interaction terms, fixed effects, a logistic propensity model used for probabilities, a matching loop, and numerical optimisation with `@`. Nothing earlier in the book teaches logarithms. Several claims do not match the chapter's own numbers: "twice the size" is really 1.62×, "margin is roughly flat" is wrong, the placebo date differs from the exercise, and "no pre-trend" sits beside a −5.1-point quarter −1.
Size: ~1,270 extracted lines, 30 book pages (pp. 161–190), 20 code blocks in the text plus 13 answer blocks, 18 exercises. Time needed stated: 10–14 hours over two weeks. Plausible? No. It fits a reader who already knows regression, logs and optimisation. With the build-up steps below (logs primer, interaction/fixed-effects walk-through, logistic-to-probability step, split matching and synthetic-control cells), 15–18 hours in two parts is realistic (31.36).

## What works (keep)
- The design of §31.1: three generated datasets with known true effects (−8%, +7%, +6 pts), so the reader sees every method miss by a little and the naive ones miss by a lot (+1.7%, −23%, +75%). Keep the "mark each method's homework" framing.
- The 2×2 DiD table followed by the same number from `smf.ols`. The coefficients map exactly to the table cells (Intercept 7.7475 = others-before; treated −0.1318; is_after 0.0900; interaction −0.0729). This is the ideal bridge; it only needs the mapping stated (31.3).
- The honesty machinery: the bandwidth table, the bunching check, the caliper sensitivity (answer 9: +0.4 / +7.1 / +12.2%), the synthetic-control placebo read "fit column first" (answer 11), the region-trend answer (13), and "four regions is not many".
- §31.9's four-sentence write-up and the "In the real world" meeting. Both are model business communication and should stay (after fixing the margin claim, 31.26).

# Ch 44 — Capstone: An End-to-End Data Science Project
Verdict: A short, readable capstone with the right central lesson: rank by expected value, not probability. It shows that a value-based list captures about 3× the value of a probability-only list with the same model, and it models a good executive memo with a limitations paragraph. The problems are in the decision logic and the claimed back-references. `CALL_COST` and `call_cost` are defined but never used, so the "per-account break-even rule from section 44.3" that two exercises depend on doesn't exist. "Value at risk" is expected lost *margin*, yet the memo calls it "revenue we'd expect to lose" and "value protected". And the "February 2026" call list is built from the 1,000 historical validation accounts whose 2025 outcome is already known. Several "Chapter N taught this" claims are wrong: Ch 36's five framing rows, Ch 39's ₹1,500 cost, Ch 45/52 in "Where this leads", and the Ch 38/40/41 rows of the toolkit table.
Size: ~590 lines / 15 pages (316–330). Time needed stated: 4–6 hours; plausible? Yes for reading, re-running the code, and exercises 1–4. The "your own end-to-end pass" project (new question, framing, reused model, function, memo) is another 6–10 hours; say so separately.

## What works (keep)
- The central demonstration (§44.4): the same model and the same 40 calls give ₹1,156,056 when ranked by value vs ₹374,379 by probability, with only 10 of 40 accounts in common. It's concrete, verified, and memorable.
- The reuse structure: §44.2 recaps Ch 37's exact pipeline and reproduces its 0.788 AUC, which proves continuity rather than re-teaching it. The "Predict before you run this" and "Changed-line exercise" prompts are good Jupyter-style habits.
- The one-page memo (§44.7): it leads with a plain-English meaning of AUC ("separates… about 79% of the time when compared pairwise", which is the correct interpretation), traces every number to a printed output, and ends with a "What this list is not" paragraph pointing to Ch 30 experiments. The real-world box (Farah's plant shutdown) teaches that model output and local knowledge complement each other.
- The capacity sweep (20/40/80 → ₹0.86M/₹1.16M/₹1.52M) shows diminishing returns clearly.

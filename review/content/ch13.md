# Ch 13 — SQL for Real Analysis
Verdict: One of the strongest teaching chapters so far. Every query is shown with its real output, most have a "How it works" breakdown, and every number I recomputed matches (to within rounding). The problems are about order and completeness, not quality. The recursive CTE in the MySQL section comes before it is taught (M.7). Running totals depend on the default window frame, which is explained two subsections later. One narrative claim contradicts its own table ("under 80% … through July"). Temporary tables and a working CREATE VIEW (output, how to check it, how to replace it) are promised but never shown. §13.7 packs ten patterns into one sitting.
Size: ~1,930 lines / 34 pages (pp. 154–187), 34 body code blocks + 13 answer blocks. Time needed stated: 15–20 hours over three weeks; plausible? Yes. Ten patterns, 16 exercises and a project fit that range if §13.7 is split into two sittings (see 13.10).

## What works (keep)
- The two plain-English pictures (recipe card = CTE, train window = window function) are carried through the whole chapter. Figure 13.1 (GROUP BY vs PARTITION BY on the same 5 rows) and Figure 13.4 (gaps-and-islands on one customer) are exactly the "worked by hand" step the author wants.
- Honest traps shown with real output: window in WHERE (error shown), ROWS vs RANGE on the three 4 May orders, LAG skipping a missing month (Pattern 5, "wrong" result shown first, then the fix), right-censoring in cohorts, filter-before-share in Pattern 1.
- Numbers reconcile across the chapter: the pivot's quarter totals, Ex 6 segment totals, Pattern 1 shares, YTD, the MoM table and Ex 16's December total all tie to ₹4,335,471 and to each other. The year-end review table re-uses them correctly.
- The "Where else you'll use it" line and the business reading after each pattern ("Reading it") teach judgment, not just syntax.

# Ch 30 — Inference & Experiments
Verdict: This is a strong, honest experimentation chapter. The one-page test plan, the sample-ratio check that fails and is traced to a Safari tagging bug, the novelty split, the peeking simulation and the write-up template are exactly what a senior analyst should teach. The numbers are right: I recomputed every statistic whose inputs are printed (Welch t, CIs, two-proportion z, chi-square and expected counts, Cramér's V, sample sizes, power, SRM, segment and per-channel splits) and reran all seven seeded simulations; all match. The main defects are these. §30.11 is the book's first use of statsmodels regression, and it cites a non-existent "Chapter 19" regression. It never explains the formula syntax (`~`, `+`, `C()`, `[T.…]`, `Treatment()`) or what odds and log-odds are. The model write-up table says "35 enquiries a month" where the chapter's own figure is ~560. The "20% lift on tablet" is actually +35%. Three analyses break the chapter's own unit-of-analysis rule (ANOVA, novelty, answer 9). It re-teaches much of Ch 22 while claiming not to.
Size: ~1,410 extracted lines, 35 pages (pp. 126–160), 28 code blocks + 12 answer blocks. Time needed stated: 14–18 hours over three weeks. Plausible? Yes for the current text, for a reader who has done Ch 22. With the regression/logistic teaching, by-hand steps and extra cells below, 18–22 hours over three weeks, in four sittings (30.40).

## What works (keep)
- The experiment spine: plan (§30.8, with MDE and sample size computed in §30.7, so "the duration came from the arithmetic, not the calendar") → assemble at the visitor grain → SRM check first → primary metric with absolute and relative intervals → guardrails → segments labelled exploratory. It is a model any team could adopt.
- Failures demonstrated rather than asserted. The SRM fails (p = 0.0003) and a crosstab by browser finds Safari at 53/47. Peeking daily turns 5% into 25.5% (reproduced exactly). Twenty A/A t-tests give 2 "significant" (reproduced). Tukey rejects nothing on a 0.04-page gap. These are memorable and correct.
- Effect size taught against p-values with a clean demonstration (d = 0.02, p ≈ 3e-10 on 200,000 per group), and the "report effect + interval + n" sentence.
- The Real World story (the agency's "+36% on Wednesday") and exercise answers 15–18 teach decision-making under uncertainty in plain business language.

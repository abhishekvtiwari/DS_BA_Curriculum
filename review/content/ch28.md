# Ch 28 — Advanced SQL, Performance & Data Modeling

Verdict: This is one of the strongest chapters in the book. Every result reconciles (the BOM cost, the org counts, the star schema against `sales_lines`, "as it was" against "as it is"), the performance section measures before and after, and the SCD and migration material is what practitioners actually do. For a first-time reader, though, it has four serious problems. (1) Recursion is not taught from zero. The section opens "Chapter 13 used WITH RECURSIVE once…", and that use is being removed, so the first recursive query the reader sees is a 5-level org chart. (2) The large database and `migrate.py` need the psql client, a terminal and credentials that are never set up. (3) The type 2 load closes versions with `valid_to = 2026-03-31` under half-open joins, which leaves a one-day gap. This contradicts the chapter's own rule and the `dw` build. (4) Several blocks do too much at once: the `dim_customer` build, the first frames query, and experiments 2–3, whose code or plans are missing. The chapter also re-teaches the star schema as if new (Ch 16 §16.3 already taught it), and calls it something "Chapter 16 builds on".
Size: 3,577 extracted lines, about 81 pages (Part III pp. 4–84). About 75 SQL blocks in the body plus 22 answers. Time needed stated: 18–24 hours over three to four weeks. Plausible? Only just, and only without the project. It covers six topics that are each a chapter elsewhere (recursion, advanced windows, plans and indexes, normalization and grain, dimensional modeling and SCDs, migrations), plus generating and loading a 1.9-million-line database. It needs explicit sittings (28.26). With the project, 24–30 hours is more honest.

## What works (keep)
- Reconciliation is part of the method everywhere: 35 rows = `COUNT(*) FROM staff`; people_below 34 = 35 − 1; ₹647.80 checked by hand in Figure 28.2 and the text; star schema = ₹4,335,471 and 326 lines = `sales_lines`; as-it-was vs as-it-is difference ₹84,842 explained by two customers' orders; revenue identical in 1NF and 3NF.
- The performance story teaches with measured before/after medians, "timings are measurements, not facts", and a reusable checklist. The real-world case (Meera's dashboard) ties every fix to its cause, including the one trade-off (staleness) put to the decision owner.
- The SCD section is excellent practice: point-in-time join, the business-key fan-out trap with real numbers (₹173,000 vs ₹95,500), the partial unique index that enforces one current row, and "say which view the report uses, in its title".
- Migrations are built by hand first (a history table, checksums), then mapped to Flyway conventions. Expand/contract, and MySQL's silent `''` for a NOT NULL column, are exactly the right warnings.

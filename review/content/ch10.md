# Ch 10 — Spreadsheet Fundamentals: Excel & Google Sheets
Verdict: A strong, practical chapter. Riverstone's 330-line export is used consistently, almost every formula result is shown, the numbers reconcile to the rupee, and the "check what a cell contains" habit is taught well. The main problems for a first-time reader: (1) setup is three lines inside §10.1, with no first-run check (approved §10.0 is missing), and the "Back to Chapter 4" exercises aren't here yet; (2) formulas and functions are used in §10.3–§10.5 before §10.6/§10.7 teach `=` and functions, and one of those early formulas points at the wrong column; (3) a wrong count (135 vs 125 "wrong real dates") repeats in four places; (4) the chapter cites "Chapter 13" as the source of numbers the reader already met in Chapter 4, and it has four SQL/pandas preview boxes (M.10).
Size: 1,551 extracted lines, about 33 pages (pp. 8–40). ~50 functions, 75 key terms, 29 exercises, a 9-step project. Time needed stated: 14–17 hours over two weeks. Plausible? No, it's optimistic. For a true beginner the content alone is ~16–20 h (29 exercises plus a project that takes 3–4 h). The approved §10.0 and the Chapter 4 exercises add another ~2 h. Restate as **18–22 hours over two to three weeks** and give a sitting plan (see 10.22).

## What works (keep)
- §10.3 "What a cell really contains", with the Cell detective sheet (2900 vs "2900" vs "2900␣", date as 45659) and the COUNT-vs-COUNTA watch-out. It's exactly the habit a beginner needs, and it pays off in Meera's story.
- §10.4 measures the CSV damage on real data (January ₹202,640 → ₹91,649) and gives the fix in both apps. The story replays the same diagnosis step by step.
- The reconciliation habit runs all the way through: rep split and segment split add back to ₹4,335,471; the tracker check row = 0; answer 29's ₹62,650 = the two cancelled orders. Every figure I recomputed matched, except the 135/125 count (10.3).
- Most formulas show their result inline (`→ 4398121`), and the first formula (§10.6) is checked by hand (45 × ₹750 × 0.95 = ₹32,062.50). XLOOKUP's four arguments (§10.9) are explained one by one. This is the model the rest of the chapter should follow.

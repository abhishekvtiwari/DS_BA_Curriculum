# Ch 60 — Designing Whole Systems
Verdict: A strong, well-paced conceptual chapter. Definitions are plain, the C4, NFR and ADR ideas each get a Riverstone example, and the story models good judgement. The biggest problems are consistency with Part V. The warehouse is called "Postgres + Delta", but Part V built a DuckDB warehouse, and Postgres there is the ERP source. The freshness and Flash-timing NFRs cite Chapter 47 and Chapter 20 numbers that those chapters do not contain. The ADR numbering and dates contradict each other. The PO-intake NFR, as written, would allow straight-through loading, which the chapter's own ADR-021 rejects. "p95" is used but never defined.
Size: ~815 lines / 20 pages; Time needed stated: 12–15 hours over two weeks; plausible? Yes. About 3 hours of reading, plus a project that needs a design document, two diagrams, five or more NFRs and three ADRs (8–10 hours).

## What works (keep)
- §60.1's working definition, "expensive to change later and affects more than one part", with its three-property test and the variable-name vs Dagster contrast. It is exactly the plain idea → concrete example progression the book wants.
- The adjective → question → number table (§60.3) and the filled-in ADR-014 (Figure 60.5). Both are reusable templates tied to a real Part V incident.
- The end-to-end trace of one PO email across the container diagram (§60.5). It makes "a diagram is documentation only if you can trace a request across it" concrete.
- The story (the rewrite that didn't happen) shows the evolve-don't-rewrite pattern with named, fixable gaps.

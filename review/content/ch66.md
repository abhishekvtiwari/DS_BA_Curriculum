# Ch 66 — Data Strategy, Maturity & Building Data Teams
Verdict: A thoughtful leadership chapter. The one-page strategy, "show the measured floor, name the unmeasured ceiling" and "hire against a named gap" are genuinely useful and well told. But the chapter's own logic fails at its centre. The governance hire is justified by a maturity score that ranks governance **second-best** (3.4), not weakest. The ROI code crashes (IndexError printed as output). The ₹9 lakh hire the business case is written for is never weighed against anything. The Daily Flash saving (₹97,500) contradicts Chapter 63's ₹3.9 lakh for the same 325 hours, and on Chapter 63's rate the platform already pays for itself, which would reverse the chapter's headline. Several cross-references point to the wrong chapter or section.
Size: ~627 lines / 16 pages (pp. 121–136). Time needed stated: 8–10 hours over a week. Plausible? Yes: light code, mostly reading and the project.

## What works (keep)
- §66.1's five-part one-page strategy, with "a strategy that doesn't say no to something isn't a strategy". Concrete, testable, memorable.
- §66.3's "measured floor / unmeasured ceiling" framing and the CFO-proof sentence. It is excellent practical teaching and it carries straight into Ch 67's board speech.
- §66.4's hiring principle ("a team that's busy building things nobody asked for is not understaffed; it's misdirected").
- §66.6 recasts earlier stories (Ch 19 nine-year macro, Ch 63 shadow-IT audit, Ch 62 vendor pitch) as change-management lessons. Good use of the book's continuity.

# Ch 55 — Building AI Applications: RAG, Agents & Evaluation
Verdict: A strong, honest chapter. Every retrieval claim is backed by a table, refusal is treated as a business trade-off, and the "assistant as auditor of your documentation" story is excellent. For a first-time reader it has three big problems. (1) Most of the working code sits in companion modules the reader never sees (`retrieval.py`, `assistant.py`, `tools.py`: `tokenize`, `rank`, `build_chunks`, `VectorIndex`, `SupportAssistant`, `propose_tool_call`, `run_tool_call`, `PRICES`, `DISCOUNTS`), so several of the key results come out of black boxes. (2) Some of the chapter's own conclusions don't match its code or data: `chunk_sections` does not attach the title the text says it does; "just use embeddings is contradicted" rests on a TF-IDF+SVD stand-in; the cost per question is about 13 paise, not "a fraction of a paisa". (3) There is no setup step: the corpus is never generated, and new regex constructs arrive untaught.
Size: ~1,190 extracted lines, about 29 PDF pages (68–96 of Part VI). Time needed stated: 16–20 hours over three weeks. Plausible? Just, for the chapter as printed. Once the hidden modules are shown and explained cell by cell (which this review asks for), 20–24 hours is more honest. Add a three-sitting plan: 55.1–55.5 retrieval; 55.6–55.8 grounding, tools and agents; 55.9–55.11 evaluation, guardrails and shipping.

## What works (keep)
- Every design choice is measured on the same 65-question set: the 9-row chunking × search table (Fig 55.2), the refusal threshold sweep (Fig 55.3) and the four-rung evaluation ladder (Fig 55.4). A beginner learns to measure rather than follow advice.
- The superseded delivery policy is planted in the corpus, surfaces in 55.4, is fixed in 55.10 and returns in the real-world story. It is one clear thread through the whole chapter.
- The point about absolute versus normalized scores for refusal (55.6) is a genuinely useful implementation lesson. The "Agents, without the hype" section (55.8) gives sound, sober guidance.
- The end story ("refusal log = content backlog", the 12 unanswered questions that became 3 FAQs) makes the business case better than any metric could.

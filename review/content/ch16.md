# Ch 16 — Business Intelligence with Power BI
Verdict: This is professional, current content: accurate 2026 licensing, the right modelling doctrine, SQL cross-checks for every card, and a strong real-world story. For a first-time reader it has four blocking problems. (1) There is no install section. (2) The fact table is never actually built: the chapter wavers between importing raw `orders` + `order_items` and using the `sales_lines` view, then writes measures against columns that don't exist in either version. (3) Several DAX blocks are wrong or would print the wrong thing: the crore format string, the "Last Refreshed" measure, the RLS "no role sees all" claim, and the Answer 22 measure. (4) DAX is delivered in batches of 7–8 measures with no per-measure output. Under D1 there is no pandas code at all in the chapter, and only four sentences need changing.
Size: ~1,120 lines, ~23 pages (pp. 247–269). Time needed stated: 18–22 hours over two to three weeks. Plausible? Just, for reading and building the Desktop parts. With the new §16.0 install, step-by-step DAX and the Service/RLS work, 22–26 hours is more honest. Many learners will also be unable to do §16.9–16.10 at all without a work or school account (16.24).

## What works (keep)
- "Check your measures against SQL": every card has an expected value and the SQL that produces it (`expected_measures.md`, `ch16_checks.sql`). This is exactly the verification habit a beginner needs. Nearly all values recompute.
- Licensing is accurate as of Sept 2026 (Pro $14, PPU $24 from 1 April 2025, F2 ≈ $263/month, F64 free viewers, P SKUs retired), and it carries a "check current rules" caveat.
- The real-world story ("₹2.3 crore that vanished") ties Ch 14's missing-city data and Ch 15's "show the gap" rule to a BI trust failure, with numbers that reconcile (₹112.4 cr + ₹2.3 cr = ₹114.7 cr).
- The "mistakes that produce wrong numbers" table (grain, averaged %, cancelled orders, blank categories) is the most valuable page in the chapter.

# Ch 64 — Security, Privacy, Governance & Responsible AI
Verdict: Well-argued and readable, with a strong worked fairness audit (gap, then mechanism) and an honest "not legal advice" stance. But it breaks promises made by earlier chapters: no GRANT/REVOKE (promised by Ch 12), no model cards, explainability or AI disclosure (promised by Ch 53–58), and nothing on pasting data into AI assistants (Ch 6, 26). Its central artefact, the access matrix (Figure 64.1), contradicts both its own prose and the Chapter 60 NFR it claims to close. Several legal statements (DPDP phasing, GDPR scope, the EU AI Act omnibus) need checking by a lawyer. The first-time reader also meets PII, data principal, disparate impact and hashing without definitions.
Size: ~835 lines / 21 pages (pp. 83–103). Time needed stated: 14–16 hours over two weeks. Plausible? Only if the project is included: reading plus exercises is about 6–8 h. The one code section is two short blocks, and adding the proposed GRANT/row-level-security section would justify the upper end.

## What works (keep)
- §64.7's two-step audit: prove the gap is real (Welch t-test), then look for the mechanism by comparing leads of the same company size. It is the chapter's best teaching and shows why "remove the feature" is the wrong fix. The five-step generalized method is reusable.
- §64.1's three design-time questions and §64.9's "closing a named risk from Chapter 60" give the chapter a concrete purpose and tie it into the Riverstone story.
- The "In plain English" office-badge analogy maps security, privacy, governance and responsible AI cleanly onto one picture.
- The "Common mistakes" table and the "treat regulation dates as perishable" habit (warm-up Q3, mistake "Treating a regulation's original deadline as current").

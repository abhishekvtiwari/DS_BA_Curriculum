# Ch 72A — Data Structures & Algorithms Question Bank
Verdict: A useful, well-sized DSA bank for data roles. Every snippet runs and prints what the chapter says (all re-run with python3 3.x; outputs match). The big problem is alignment. Every "Learn it in" points to Chapter 72 (the Python & pandas *question bank*, which teaches nothing), when the teaching chapter is Ch 33, *The Computer Science You Actually Need*. About a third of the bank (linked lists, BSTs, level-order, merge sort, sliding window, Floyd's cycle detection, hand-written classes) is never taught anywhere in the book. The chapter also opens with a drafting note to "the coordinator", and two of its code samples break rules the book itself teaches (a list as a BFS `visited` set; a mutable default `cache={}`, which is Q72-001's gotcha).
Size: ~730 extracted lines, 17 pages (pp. 109–125), 30 questions (14 core + 16 rapid-fire). Time needed stated: none. Plausible? No estimate to judge. Other chapters give one, and the bank should too: about 6–8 hours to work every snippet and say each answer aloud, plus 4–6 hours if the reader also has to learn the untaught topics (see 72A.3).

## What works (keep)
- Measured, reproducible demonstrations. The O(n²) vs O(n) duplicate check (Q72A-002) and naive vs memoized Fibonacci (Q72A-018) show real timings. My re-run: 0.285 s vs 0.00074 s (≈390×); fib(28) 0.037 s vs 0.000019 s (≈1,950×). The ratios differ by machine, as the text says, and the lesson holds.
- The complexity table in Q72A-001: "5,000 items, roughly" ~13 / 5,000 / ~61,000 / 25,000,000 steps. All correct, and a concrete way to feel growth rates.
- Karan's real-world story (sets → external sort + two-pointer merge when the data doesn't fit in memory). It carries the [Scale] move and links to Q72A-012 and Ch 77.
- The spoken walk-through in Q72A-030 (bracket validation) models the "say it live" habit from Ch 69, with the edge cases (empty string, ")))", ")(") named. All three verified.

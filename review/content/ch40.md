# Ch 40 — Time Series & Forecasting
Verdict: Content, data and narrative are excellent. The chapter uses a seasonal-naive baseline from the start, backtesting shows SARIMA's 2025 win shrinking over five folds, the planner story is honest, and the sensor section finds a real "slow drift" insight. Most numbers recompute. The first-time-reader standard is not met. Autocorrelation (claimed as taught in Ch 21) is never taught anywhere before this chapter. AIC, the SARIMAX summary table and the ADF statistic are used unexplained. WAPE is used five sections before it is worked by hand. There is no tiny by-hand example for decomposition, moving average, ACF, MAPE/WAPE/MASE or Holt's trend. Several code blocks do 4–8 new things at once. Factual problems:
- The Chapter 48 claim about "12 machines and 40 weeks … with PySpark, DuckDB, and Polars" and "the same generator" is false. Ch 48 uses 25 machines, 10-second readings, a different generator, and no anomaly rules.
- "Chapter 45 business metrics" and "Chapter 52 monitoring" are wrong references.
- The residual "largest" calculation has a bug.
- The (1,1,1)(0,1,1) model is called the most common, but the airline model (0,1,1)(0,1,1) holds that title.
- Answers 2, 7 and 12 misread or cannot support their own premise.

Size: ~1,375 lines / 35 pages (196–230 of 330). Time needed stated: 10–14 hours over two weeks. Plausible? No, not for a first-time reader. The chapter carries ~55 key terms, three new libraries (statsmodels time-series, Prophet with CmdStan install, `ewm`/time-based rolling) and a two-part subject (forecasting + sensor anomalies). Once the by-hand steps are added, 15–20 hours is realistic. Suggest four sittings: (1) 40.1–40.3 components, stationarity, baselines; (2) 40.4–40.5 smoothing and SARIMA; (3) 40.6–40.9 ML, backtesting, metrics, plan; (4) 40.10 sensors + project. Alternatively, move 40.10 into Ch 50 (streaming), where alerts are built.

## What works (keep)
- Baseline-first discipline. Seasonal naive (10.0%) is shown beating Holt–Winters (17.5%), and the four-category table shows no method wins everywhere. This is exactly the honesty the book is built on.
- Backtesting (40.7) turns SARIMA's 4.0% into a five-year mean of 11.3%, with the 2021 fold explained. The safety-stock plan is then sized from the backtest error, and answer 9 exposes Prophet's 33% miss on 2021.
- Simple exponential smoothing worked by hand in code, one step per line (every level recomputes ✓).
- The sensor section's central lesson ("point-by-point tests can't see slow drift") is demonstrated, not asserted: the z-score is +2.8 at the hottest minute, while the 24-h median baseline with a persistence rule catches the fault. Answer 13's threshold × window table is a good trade-off exercise.

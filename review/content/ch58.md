# Ch 58 — Intelligent Automation
Verdict: One of the most valuable chapters in the book for a working analyst: it puts writing to a system of record (UNIQUE key, transaction, audit log), a priced approval threshold, an exception taxonomy, "straight-through rate and silent error rate are the same system described two ways", and an honest ROI table in one place, and its central finding (88% straight through, 19% of those silently wrong) is exactly right. But the chapter contradicts its own finding in several places (the "In plain English" framing, the Recap, the real-world story and answer 10 contradict §58.7–58.8), its break-even arithmetic is wrong by about ten times (the "~2% silent error" gate and answers 9, 11 and 15 do not survive the chapter's own cost model), and the pipeline itself (`intake.run`, `erp.py`) is never shown — the INSERTs are literally `(...)` — while `run(emails, truth)` quietly passes the ground truth into the production pipeline.
Size: ~980 extracted lines, 23 pages (pp. 145–167), Time needed stated: 14–18 hours over three weeks; plausible? Yes for reading + 18 exercises + the 10-step project, if `intake.py`/`erp.py` are walked through in the chapter; without that the project (build the pipeline) is under-supported. Suggest sittings: §58.1–58.4 (choose, assemble, write safely), §58.5–58.8 (gates, exceptions, measurement, ROI, rollout), §58.9–58.10 + project.

## What works (keep)
- §58.4's three properties of a safe write, each with the mechanism in the database (UNIQUE `source_email`, `with connection:` transaction, audit rows naming the pipeline version as actor), and the replay proof. "Put the guarantee in the database, because application-level checks lose races and get refactored away."
- §58.7 "The same system, described two ways": 88% straight-through beside 19% silent error, then priced — the labour-plus-error-cost table that shows the cheapest-looking option is the most expensive. Figure 58.2's caption "The second panel is the one missing from most business cases" is the chapter's best line.
- §58.8's segment analysis ("automate the segment you are excellent at, not everything you are mediocre at") with the counter-intuitive result that tables, not prose alone, are weak; §58.10's "the input could be structured instead — ask the five biggest customers for a template".
- §58.9 and the real-world story on the people side: talk to the coordinator first, make her the queue owner, measure corrections caught rather than orders processed, and "the interface is part of the automation".

# Ch 51 — Data Activation: Reverse ETL, APIs & System Integration
Verdict: The ideas are strong and well chosen: pull vs push, a system of record for each field, PATCH vs PUT, reconciling writes, and the honest RPA/EDI section. The closing story is one of the best in Part V. As printed, though, the chapter's code is broken. **Every one of the seven executed cells shows `NameError`** because §51.2 uses `sl` and `scores` before the setup cell in §51.3 defines them. Every "Reading it" paragraph therefore describes output the reader never sees, and the one visible worked example (lead 2) contradicts itself. Three more problems are serious. The core computations (`lead_scores`, `overdue_flags`, `idempotency_key`) are hidden in a companion module. The content-only idempotency key has a real bug: a value that goes back to an earlier value can never be re-sent. Several things the chapter promises are never taught: rate limits, upserts, timeout retries, secrets and HMAC.
Size: ~866 lines / 21 pp (160–180). Time needed stated: 12–16 hours over two to three weeks. Plausible? Yes for the current text plus the 9-step project. Once the missing SQL, the key function, the PATCH/PUT demo, a webhook receiver and HMAC are added, 14–18 h is more honest.

## What works (keep)
- §51.1 explains pull vs push ("A dashboard is pulled… A synced field is pushed") clearly, and the fields table ("Computed from / Changes what") gives the business reason for every sync.
- §51.5 names a system of record per field, not per system. The "computed downstream vs entered upstream by a person" distinction is exactly what a BA needs. The 4-step minimal conflict policy is concrete.
- The closing story (a PUT that erased a negotiated discount, found through an audit trail) ties together PATCH/PUT, system of record, reconciliation and the audit trail, and the "What made this work" list is accurate.
- §51.10 handles RPA and EDI/SFTP with maturity ("still normal, not a failure"). It is consistent with Ch 7 §7.4 and Ch 63 §63.3.

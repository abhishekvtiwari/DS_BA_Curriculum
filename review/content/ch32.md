# Ch 32 — Analytics Engineering with dbt
Verdict: A clear, practical chapter that explains why analytics engineering matters. It has the kitchen-rota analogy, the reconciliation test, "build, not run then test", and the two-dashboards story. As an explainer it works. As a build-along a first-timer cannot finish it. Jinja is used from §32.2 but defined only in §32.11. YAML goes well beyond Ch 26's primer. The install commands (`uv add`, then a bare `dbt`) fail as written. The §32.5 `dbt build` output depends on models, a snapshot, an incremental model and a test that come three to five sections later, and several are never shown. The snapshot reads a `raw_crm.customers` table that no chapter creates. The CI workflow cannot pass: it uses a `ci` target that doesn't exist, psql has no password, `dbt deps` runs after the lint that needs it, and a load script is never shown. The Ch 34 prerequisite becomes Ch 26 §26.0 under D2. That covers most commands, but not `rm -rf`, psql's flags, running `./script.sh` or `localhost:8080`.
Size: ~1,390 extracted lines, 33 book pages (pp. 191–223), ~36 code/config blocks plus 8 answer blocks, 18 exercises. Time needed stated: 16–20 hours over three weeks. Plausible? Roughly, for a reader who already knows YAML, Jinja and the terminal. With the primers and missing files added (32.4–32.9), 18–22 hours in three sittings (32.36).

## What works (keep)
- The motivation: "Why this matters" names four concrete problems of Ch 28's hand-built star (order, checks, dependencies, history), and the kitchen-rota "In plain English" maps each to a dbt feature.
- `dbt compile` shown early (§32.3), turning Jinja and the macro back into readable SQL, with "everything dbt runs is SQL you can read". That is the right mental model.
- The reconciliation test and the failure demo (§32.7). The model and its downstream tests are 8 nodes, and the output matches that count. Answer 8's point that a test built on the same macro cannot catch a change to that macro is excellent.
- Figure 32.3 (build vs run-then-test), the "How much to test" table, and the two-dashboards story ("dbt didn't settle the argument; writing the definition down did").

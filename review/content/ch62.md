# Ch 62 — Data Architecture Patterns
Verdict: The judgement it teaches ("fit over fashion", the honest mesh scorecard, contracts and semantic layer as the glue) is excellent and readable. The central worked example has the wrong facts. The "one real scenario" (Taloja sensors → line alert + warehouse) is exactly what Chapter 50 §50.8 built, as a streaming speed path plus a daily batch correction path, which is essentially Lambda. The chapter instead describes the defect-*image* model's "edge inference", a different system, and calls it neither pattern. The semantic layer is credited throughout to Chapter 23 (it is taught in Chapter 32). Figure and section numbers are tangled, and the maturity "scorecard" has no scores.
Size: ~800 lines / 19 pages; Time needed stated: 10–12 hours over a week; plausible? Yes. No code; about 2.5 h of reading plus a substantial written project (comparison, scorecard, data-product check, recommendation).

## What works (keep)
- The two independent axes (how data is processed vs who owns it) are stated early (§62.1) and reinforced in the mistakes table. They prevent a very common confusion.
- Medallion is presented as a new *name* for Ch 45's raw/staging/modeled layers, with a bug-type per tier (Answer 2). This is a perfect "you already know this" bridge.
- Figure 62.5 (the same "active_customer" with and without a contract) and the four-property data-product test are concrete and memorable.
- The mesh-pitch story shows a scored "not yet" with a named trigger, and the trigger is later seen to fire.

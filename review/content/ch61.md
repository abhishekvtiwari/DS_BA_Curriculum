# Ch 61 — Distributed Systems & Trade-offs
Verdict: Clear and well-motivated. The wedding analogy, the "per-operation CAP choice" framing and the container-by-container failure analysis are excellent for a first-time reader. The main problems are technical precision and back-references. Idempotency is used in §61.2 but defined in §61.6, and it is credited to the wrong chapters. Partitioning is taught as sharding. "Postgres read replicas" and a Postgres warehouse don't exist in Part V. CAP consistency is mixed up with transaction atomicity, and snapshot with read-your-writes. The one code block has no line-by-line explanation, and its prose miscounts its own output. Quorum, consensus, CP/AP and horizontal/vertical scaling are never taught, although the interview bank (Ch 80) expects some of them.
Size: ~810 lines / 19 pages; Time needed stated: 10–12 hours over a week; plausible? Yes. About 2.5 h of reading, 1 h for the simulation and timed challenge, 6–8 h for the failure-analysis project.

## What works (keep)
- §61.2's three "what people get wrong about CAP" points, each tied to a Riverstone example (order writes choose C, dashboard reads choose A).
- The eventual-consistency simulation. It makes "eventually" visible, and the seeded output reproduces exactly (verified).
- The failure-analysis table (Figure 61.4) and the five-step method. It finds one real SPOF instead of listing eight equal risks.
- The story (CRM outage) contrasts two containers making different, correct choices for the same failure, and it ends with a chaos test.

# Ch 18 — Python for Analysts: pandas & Automation
Verdict: Strong, realistic content: the Riverstone numbers reconcile across chapters, the habits taught are the right ones (match rates, fan-out, bound parameters, checks before publishing), and 18.15 is a good model of an automated report. For a first-time reader it is not yet teachable. Three printed outputs are errors: 18.8 shows `TypeError`/`NameError` and 18.12 shows `KeyError`, so the reshaping section and the Excel read-back fail as printed. Most blocks chain 4–10 new methods with no line-by-line explanation. Several tools are used sections before they are taught: `merge` in 18.1, `lambda` and `.loc` in 18.3, JSON/APIs in 18.2, NumPy throughout. The 18.11 region chart contradicts Chapter 16, putting 51% of revenue under "Other or missing". The 125-line script in 18.15 introduces about 15 new ideas at once and needs `tabulate`, which the install line leaves out.
Size: ~1,150 lines, ~24 pages (294–317), Time needed stated: 30–35 hours over four weeks; plausible? Only for the current text. The planned additions (NumPy page M.9, §14.13 and §15.14 moved in per M.2, status codes from Ch 2) and the cell splits recommended below will take the chapter to about 30 pages. At that size 35–40 hours is honest. Split the four weeks into named sittings (see 18.40).

## What works (keep)
- Reconciliation as the teaching spine. 18.6, 18.9, 18.10, 18.13 and the answers tie back to Chapters 14–16 to the rupee (₹423,561,010.50; ₹1,146,641,651.25; 22.7% October growth; segment medians). Recomputed below and consistent.
- The merge section (18.7) teaches measuring the match rate with `indicator=True` and shows a deliberate fan-out (10 → 11 rows). This is exactly the habit that separates analysts.
- 18.15 as a finished automation: arguments, environment variables, checks that stop publication, logging, exit codes, both machine and human outputs. The "In the real world" story shows why the checks matter.
- The "When pandas isn't the answer" table and the common-mistakes table are practical and honest.

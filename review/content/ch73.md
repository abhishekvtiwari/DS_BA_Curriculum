# Ch 73 — Statistics, Probability & Experimentation Bank
Verdict: Numerically the strongest bank I've checked. I recomputed every number (Bayes, Monty Hall, birthday, Type II rates, sample size, SRM chi-squares, 1 − 0.95²⁰, the z-test, the chi-square of independence, Simpson's rates, the confounded correlation), and all of them reproduce with SciPy 1.17.1 / statsmodels 0.15.0. The problems are editorial and pedagogical. Every "Learn it in" is a placeholder ("Chapter 21 or nearby … exact number to confirm"), and the chapter says "this chat doesn't have…", although Ch 21, 22, 30 and 31 exist and teach almost all of it at known sections. The closing pointer "Chapter 22 (experimentation and causal inference)" should be 30/31. Several questions test things the book never teaches (Monty Hall, birthday problem, partial correlation, holdout groups). One walk-through (Q73-036) misses its own point: the test it praises has 5,000 per arm when the chapter's own power calculation needs 8,537, for exactly that effect.
Size: ~900 extracted lines, 17 pages (pp. 126–142), 37 questions (12 core + 25 rapid-fire/walk-through). Time needed stated: none. Plausible? No estimate to judge. Suggest 8–10 hours (the simulations take time to run and understand).

## What works (keep)
- Every headline claim is a small, runnable demonstration: peeking inflating false positives (0.163 vs 0.05), SRM (5,200/4,800 → p = 0.0001), underpowered tests (Type II 0.80 at n = 30), and a constructed Simpson's paradox (35.5% vs 74.5%). This is the right way to make interview answers stick.
- The tier tables consistently separate "statistically significant" from "worth shipping" (Q73-014, Q73-036). The business framing of MDE as a decision, not a statistic (Q73-021), is excellent.
- Farah's real-world SRM story is internally consistent. Treatment loses slow mobile users, control ends up larger (5,340 vs 4,660), and the users excluded are the least likely to convert, which inflates treatment. It's a memorable reason to run the SRM check first.
- The "Common mistakes" table maps each mistake to the question that demonstrates it.

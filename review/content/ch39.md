# Ch 39 — Evaluation, Tuning, Interpretation & Honesty
Verdict: A strong, honest, business-first chapter. Its numbers are real and nearly all recompute exactly, and it gives the right lessons: use a cost-based threshold, a top-N table and calibration before quoting probabilities, and treat fairness as a policy choice. For a first-time reader it moves too fast. Every metric is introduced by running scikit-learn on the 2,225-lead validation set, with no tiny by-hand example first. Most code blocks have no line-by-line explanation. The title promises "Tuning", but the chapter contains no tuning. Permutation importance is promised by Ch 37 and listed here in Tools and Key terms, but it is taught only in an exercise answer. There are also some wrong numbers and cross-references: "660 leads", "missed at twice the rate", "₹0.9 million", "three parameters", sections 39.4→39.5, Chapter 52/61, and a "city" fairness check that never happened.
Size: ~1,350 lines / 32 pages (164–195 of 330). Time needed stated: 10–14 hours over two weeks. Plausible? Only just for someone reading and running the code. For the intended first-time reader, once the by-hand examples and code explanations this review asks for are added, 14–18 hours is honest. The chapter introduces ~55 key terms across 9 sections plus a 10-step project. Suggest two sittings per week: (1) 39.1–39.3 metrics; (2) 39.4–39.6 calibration, thresholds and imbalance; (3) 39.7–39.9 interpretation, fairness and model cards; (4) the project.

## What works (keep)
- 39.5 is the best section in the chapter. Cost is set as named variables (`WORK_COST`, `WIN_VALUE`), then the chapter derives break-even, a profit curve and a capacity threshold, and compares the result in rupees with the old three-source rule. I recomputed every profit figure (9 thresholds, grid best, capacity, old rule, test set) and all match to the rupee.
- The "Reading it" paragraphs turn metrics into business sentences, e.g. "accuracy 93.5% is worse than predicting 'lost' for everyone", "PR-AUC is 4.6× random", and "the model is worth about ₹590,000 over six months at the same effort".
- The worked SHAP local explanation reconciles to the model output. Base −3.293 plus the contributions gives 0.603, which is a probability of 0.646 ✓. The chapter also explains why a zero one-hot column gets a +0.467 contribution. This is exactly the kind of check a beginner needs.
- The fairness section (39.8) and the Farah Khan story are balanced and realistic. They show base rates against equal opportunity against the feedback loop, and conclude that the fault lay in lead assignment, not in the model. The model card (39.9) is a good, reusable template.

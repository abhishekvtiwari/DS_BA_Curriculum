# Ch 68 — How Data Hiring Works
Verdict: A useful, practical chapter. It follows the hiring "gates" in order, explains the ATS well, and its four resumes matched to JDs are a real asset. For a first-time reader the biggest problems are: (1) the Farah Khan story contradicts Chapters 8 and 9 (internal move vs external referral; 6 weeks vs 12 weeks of SQL alone; "posting stays open" vs "likely to close"), and her 90-day plan needs 11–14 study hours a week, against Chapter 9's own "6 hours alongside a full-time job"; (2) Example 3's arithmetic is wrong (12/26 should be 12/24), and two resume annotations say "every must-have keyword appears in the JD's own words" when it doesn't, which undercuts the chapter's own keyword lesson; (3) "tier 1 / tier 2" is used to mean company tiers, which clashes with Chapter 8's career-tree tiers; (4) the round-to-bank table leaves out half the banks (70, 72, 72A, 76A/B, 78–80), there's a dangling "Chapter 76", and several platform claims (ATS behaviour, LinkedIn "Open to Work" privacy, profile-photo ranking) are stated as fact with no date or caveat. The approved move of Ch 8's other salary rows and growth discussion into this chapter still has to be done, and it has knock-on effects on Ch 8's exercises.
Size: 719 extracted lines, about 20 pages (pp. 6–25). No code. 10 worked examples, 4 full resumes, 5 exercises (no answers), 1 project. Time needed stated: "2–3 hours to read, then it's a reference". Plausible? Yes for reading. The project (find a real JD, score it, write a week-by-week plan, update CV and LinkedIn) is another 3–5 hours and isn't mentioned. Restate as "2–3 hours to read; allow 3–5 hours more for the project".

## What works (keep)
- §68.1's gate model ("Prepare for the gate in front of you, not the one three steps away") gives the whole of Part VIII a frame, and the Recap and mistakes table reinforce it.
- §68.2's five-minute copy-paste ATS self-test is concrete, free and correct: a beginner can run it today.
- §68.8's four JD → resume → annotation triples (fresher DA, switcher BA, mid DS, mid DE) show the "output, not duty" rule in finished form. Nothing else in the book does this.
- Examples 4 and 5 teach judgment (an inflated JD, a senior gap), not just arithmetic; the Kubernetes clarifying question in Example 4 is exactly right.

# Ch 78 — Automation & Integration Question Bank
Verdict: The judgment content is good. The tool-choice framework, "fix the process before automating", silent-failure monitoring, and the renamed-column story are practical and interview-relevant. But the chapter is disconnected from the book that teaches this material. The pointers go to other interview banks (Ch 70, 74, 76B, 77) and almost never to Ch 20 (report automation), Ch 45 §45.9 (retry with exponential backoff and jitter), Ch 51 (reverse ETL, webhooks, idempotent writes, RPA), Ch 57 (circuit breaker) or Ch 63 (tool choice). Three of the four "verified, live" code claims either crash or aren't shown. The retry function raises NameError because `time` is never imported, and `sync_lead_score` and the rate limiter never appear. Three of the four "Full design cases" are placeholders that repeat earlier questions and include a drafting leftover.
Size: ~692 lines, ~12 pages (pp. 213–224), 31 numbered items (27 distinct questions; Q78-028/030/031 repeat 006/018/001). Time needed stated: none; plausible? Not stated. Suggest "~2 hours first pass; 30 min for the 9-item final-week list".

## What works (keep)
- Q78-001's four-way framework (macro, script, low-code, RPA), with RPA as the last resort and the note that "an RPA solution should be revisited once an API exists", is exactly the judgment Ch 63 teaches.
- Q78-018 separates "stopped executing" from "executed but did nothing". The renamed-column story (a column defaulting silently to 0 inflated commissions for two pay cycles) is a memorable, realistic failure, and its lesson is systemic: validate output, don't trust "no error".
- Q78-012 names its own gap. The in-memory `set` is flagged as not production-ready, with persistence as the fix, so the Extra points teach honesty about a toy example.
- The reverse-ETL numbers agree between the rapid-fire Q78-014 and the full case Q78-029 (72 → 80, count 3).

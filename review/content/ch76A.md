# Ch 76A — Data Analyst & Data Scientist Question Bank
Verdict: A good idea, well executed in its advice: business question first, a number for impact, an honest limitation, and defending a resume metric. The DA-vs-DS framing is mature. But its worked examples, which say they are "kept consistent with" Ch 44, contradict the book. The DS project quotes Ch 37's *churn* test AUCs (0.839 vs 0.797) as a *lead* model. It says boosting won, when Ch 37 §37.12 concluded boosting does *not* win on leads and kept logistic regression. It predicts "within 30 days" where Ch 36 used 90 days. The resume-metric answer (Q76A-021) calls a 7%→12% change "improved conversion by 12%" and defends a selection-biased comparison as rigorous. Drafting leftovers remain: the coordinator renumbering note, Q76A012B, and "Q76-001" IDs for Ch 76B. Several pointers are broken (§70.11) or point to banks instead of teaching chapters.
Size: ~620 lines, 12 pages (PDF pp. 171–182), 27 questions (7 core, 19 rapid-fire incl. "012B", 1 full walk-through). Time needed stated: none; plausible? Can't judge. Realistic: 2–2.5 hours to read and drill, plus 3–4 hours for the project (two five-line summaries, a defended metric, rehearsed walkthroughs).

## What works (keep)
- Q76A-001's "one-off question vs repeatable system" distinction, with the honest overlap. It improves on "DS does ML" and matches Ch 7 §7.2 and Ch 25 §25.1's role table.
- The structure of Q76A-007 / Q76A-012: business question → method → finding → quantified impact → "I checked back a month later". The DS version adds baseline-vs-complex, a cost-based threshold (Ch 39 §39.5) and a volunteered limitation.
- Q76A-026 unpacks "used ML to improve retention" into expected-value ranking (Ch 44's core lesson) and says plainly that causation needs a holdout. It is honest and models Ch 22/30 discipline.
- The Kavita story and the "five-line summary after every project" habit are practical and memorable.

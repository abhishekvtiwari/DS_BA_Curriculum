# Ch 48 — Big Data & Distributed Compute
Verdict: The judgment content is excellent: "most big data isn't", the four questions, the vote-counting analogy, reading plans bottom-up and counting exchanges, the broadcast-join before/after, three engines agreeing to the last unit, and the "cluster slower than the laptop" story. Every row count I could recompute is exact (25 × 8,640 × 92 = 19,872,000, per-plant and per-line splits, 129,600, 794,880). The mechanics are not built from zero, though. (1) Nothing is installed: there is no `pip install pyspark polars`, no Java install or JAVA_HOME, and no first-session smoke test. (2) Polars is used as if already known ("familiar after pandas and Polars"), but no earlier chapter teaches it. (3) The chapter never shows Spark's public `explain()`. Plans come through a helper built on the private `_jdf` API and three regexes, and that helper's `split(", ")` truncates the `PartitionFilters`/`PushedFilters` lines the text then asks the reader to read. (4) The sensor dataset contradicts Ch 40, which promises "12 machines and 40 weeks… 4.8-million-row version… from the same generator" and says Ch 48 "runs this chapter's anomaly rules". Ch 48 uses a different script, 25 machines, 10-second readings, 92 days and 19,872,000 rows, and runs no anomaly rules. Two drafting leftovers remain ("The draft of this book…", "the comparison the blueprint asks for").
Size: 853 lines, about 22 book pages (97–118), 11 code blocks plus 2 in the answers, 14 exercises. Time needed stated: 12–16 hours over two to three weeks; plausible? Yes for reading and practice once the tools work. But Java/Spark setup on Windows commonly costs 1–3 hours on its own, and the missing Polars primer adds about 1 hour. Restate it as 14–18 hours, first sitting "install and first SparkSession (1–2 h)".

## What works (keep)
- §48.1's size table and four questions ("how much data does the job actually touch… after the filter"), plus the "we might need it later" Watch out. This is the judgment interviewers probe, taught before any code.
- §48.4's bottom-up plan reading with `partial_sum` → `Exchange hashpartitioning` → `HashAggregate`, and the rule "every Exchange is a shuffle; count the exchanges first". §48.5's join shows three exchanges collapsing to one with `F.broadcast()`, with correct supervisor counts (794,880 = 1 machine × 92 days × 8,640).
- §48.6 checks correctness before speed (all three engines agree: 203.43 °C, 1.33%, 23,234,149 units), and it is honest about what one-core timings can and cannot show.
- The real-world story (reading 300 M rows to answer a question about 12 M; the storage layout, not the engine, was the problem) sets up Ch 49 perfectly. Ch 49 lines 22 and 456 pick it up.

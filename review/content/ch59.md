# Ch 59 — Industry Case Studies
Verdict: A well-conceived reading chapter: the six-question frame, "read a case study for its constraints rather than its technology", the honest labelling of composites, and cases that each include what went wrong make it one of the best synthesis chapters in the book for a first-time reader. Its problems are almost all cross-reference and consistency: it claims three cases are Riverstone's measured work when only two are (Case 7 is a composite but is cited as Ch 55's "runnable version"), four chapter pointers are wrong (Ch 19 for seasonal forecasting, Ch 38 for leakage, Ch 79 for "The First 90 Days", Part VII = 60–68), Case 1's "six weeks later" contradicts Ch 53's "three weeks", and the "Five patterns" section contradicts itself ("not one is a modeling error" while listing a training-data error and leakage). It has no exercises, which a "judge your own next project" chapter needs.
Size: ~500 extracted lines, 12 pages (pp. 168–179), Time needed stated: 3–4 hours to read, plus "a useful afternoon"; plausible? Yes — 12 pages of dense tables read carefully is 2–3 hours; the afternoon mapping one's own project is realistic, but only if the chapter gives the reader a worksheet to fill (59.12).

## What works (keep)
- The six questions and Figure 59.1 ("Question 3 is the one every case study is written about. Questions 1, 2, 5 and 6 are where these nine projects were actually won or lost") — a reusable frame that ties back to Ch 5.
- "How to read a case study": find the constraint before the technology, ask for the baseline, ask when the truth arrives, read the deployment sentence; plus the "published case studies are marketing" warning.
- Every case uses the same eight-row card including "What went wrong" and "The fix", and composites are labelled "numbers are representative … rather than measured" every time. Case 7's deflection story (48% and falling satisfaction → 35% resolved-and-satisfied) and Case 8's alert budget are excellent teaching.
- Case 9 correctly reuses Chapter 58's numbers: 88% straight-through, 19% silently wrong, two hours → about half an hour, and "full automation only for the email formats that scored perfectly" (which agrees with §58.8 — Ch 58's own Recap/story should be brought into line with this, see Ch 58 findings 58.5–58.6).

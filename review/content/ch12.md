# Ch 12 — Databases & SQL Foundations
Verdict: One of the strongest chapters in the book. The data is small enough to check by eye, and every one of the ~110 printed results I rechecked matches the data. The traps (AND/OR, NULL, fan-out, ON vs WHERE, MySQL dates and collation) are taught with real, reproducible outputs. For a first-time SQL reader it has four real problems. (1) It never says how to run a query in DBeaver, and it shows psql-style output that the reader's DBeaver grid won't look like. (2) Keywords appear before their sections: ORDER BY in six queries before §12.7, JOIN in §12.9, subqueries in §12.10, date arithmetic before "Working with dates", and the MySQL monthly report before the PostgreSQL original. (3) A few early queries each bring in 3–5 new ideas at once. (4) §12.13 (DDL in two dialects) is a chapter-sized load inside an already long chapter. The four approved moves into the chapter (install without Ch 6, the March issue-tree queries, the ch07 MySQL file, the ROUND note) are specified below with verified SQL and outputs.
Size: ~3,535 extracted lines, pp. 87–153 of Part II (~67 pages). About 115 code blocks plus 29 exercises with answers. Time needed stated: 19–23 hours over 3–4 weeks. Plausible? Only for a PostgreSQL-only reader who skips the project. Rough build-up: install 1.5–2 h (both DBs); §12.1–12.2 1.5 h; §12.4–12.9 5–6 h; §12.10–12.12 4–5 h; §12.13 4–5 h in two dialects; §12.14–12.16 plus the real-world report 2.5–3 h; 29 exercises 4–5 h; project 4–8 h. That gives about 27–35 h with MySQL and the project, and 20–24 h without them. State two figures ("20–24 h core; add 6–10 h for the MySQL track and project") and mark sittings (see 12.22).

## What works (keep)
- Every result is reproducible from the 8-customer, 12-order mini database printed in §12.2, and the chapter keeps asking the reader to check by hand. Examples: order 5001 = ₹14,700; reps + unassigned = ₹323,930; ageing total = ₹100,460 = invoiced − paid; discount check 181,250 − 19,865 = 161,385. That reconcile habit is the chapter's backbone.
- Traps are shown as plausible wrong answers, not errors: the AND/OR Patel Kitchenware row, `<> 'Mumbai'` losing Sunrise Caterers, fan-out 5 vs 3 orders, ₹385,610 vs ₹297,710 invoiced, LEFT JOIN turned into INNER by WHERE, MySQL `DATE - DATE` = 126 instead of 54. Each is set up, explained and fixed. Excellent teaching.
- §12.8's ageing query is wrong for the business on purpose (invoice 9001 is paid but flagged overdue), and §12.15 Q3 fixes it. That arc ("technically correct query, wrong business answer") is exactly what analysts need.
- §12.13 teaches constraints through the purchasing brief: each spreadsheet failure becomes NOT NULL, UNIQUE, CHECK or FK, with the real error text in both databases. The live-change checklist and the Friday-evening exercise 29 are senior-level judgement written for beginners.

# Ch 81 — Behavioral, HR & Offer Conversations
Verdict: Good advice on STAR, weaknesses, questions to ask and declining gracefully, written with worked scripts rather than lists. Two serious problems undercut it. First, the story bank and worked STAR answers tell the reader to take *other people's* stories from earlier chapters as their own, in the first person: Farah's Ch 73 SRM incident, the book's own Ch 71 drafting bug "adapted to first person", Arjun's Ch 77 pipeline. That is advice to fabricate, and it contradicts the chapter's own honesty rules. Second, the offer section never mentions CTC, in-hand pay, fixed vs variable, or notice period. Ch 8 promises "Chapter 81 covers… negotiating on CTC", and Ch 68 hands the CTC/notice-period conversation to this chapter. There's also no "general information, not financial or legal advice" caveat, which Ch 8 has.
Size: ~640 lines / 12 pages (251–262). Time needed stated: none; plausible? Needs adding: 2–3 hours to read; the project (story bank + three STAR outlines + timed practice) is realistically another 4–6 hours.

## What works (keep)
- Q81-001's hook ("Action is where most of your answer's time should go. Result needs a number") and the Passes/Strong contrast are clear and actionable.
- Q81-013's weakness example is honest and specific, and the extra point about calling it "ongoing" rather than "fixed" is genuinely good coaching.
- Q81-018's three questions to ask are strong, role-specific and non-generic, especially "a recent decision where the data pointed one way and the call went another".
- Q81-024's "never state a competing number you don't actually have" and Q81-027's graceful-decline advice are correct and appropriately cautious.
- The Arjun real-world box ("your technical stories are usually your best behavioral stories") is the right thesis. It matches Ch 77's Arjun (same person, same three-week silent pipeline ✓).

# Ch 80 — Architecture & Leadership Question Bank
Verdict: A clear, well-judged senior bank. The answers are sound: CAP framed as CP vs AP, build vs buy framed by total cost of ownership and whether the capability differentiates the business, pushback framed as business risk plus an alternative, and a design case that resists over-engineering. The problems are alignment and leftovers. The chapter says its "Learn it in" pointers are chapter-level only "since this chat doesn't have their approved text". Several pointers are wrong (build vs buy → 62 instead of 66 §66.5; "warehouse-vs-operational" → 62; orchestration → 77, a question bank). Horizontal/vertical scaling, leader-follower replication and payback period are tested but never taught. The flagship design case starts from "today's single-database setup", which contradicts the platform Riverstone has had since Part V (Ch 60 §60.5).
Size: ~550 lines / 10 pages (241–250). Time needed stated: none (Ch 68 and 69 state one; Ch 70–82 don't); plausible? Needs adding: about 2–3 hours to read and drill, plus 2–3 hours for the four-step project (ADR, ROI, pushback, roadmap).

## What works (keep)
- Q80-001: "partition tolerance isn't optional, so the real choice is CP vs AP" is the correct modern framing. The bank-balance vs like-count examples match Ch 61's own exercise answer (Ch 61 line ~718), so the reader sees continuity.
- Q80-006's extra point ("is this capability core to our differentiation, or a solved problem vendors do well?") is exactly the senior-level answer. It lines up with Ch 66 §66.5.
- Q80-017's worked pushback script ("if there's a constraint I'm not seeing…") models Ch 67 §67.4's "saying no well is almost never actually saying no" pattern word for word in spirit.
- Q80-023 and the Vikram story teach the right move from fixing a bug to fixing the condition, and the arithmetic holds (1 incident + 14 unmonitored = "fifteen single points of failure" ✓).

# Visual review — Ch15-Data-Visualization-Principles.pdf (40 pages)
Verdict: The best-looking chapter in this batch. Most matplotlib charts (Figs 15.1, 15.3–15.6, 15.8–15.10, 15.12, 15.14) print at about 7–7.5 pt and read well, with clear action titles and direct labels. The problems are concentrated in the project figures. Fig 15.15 (the "old management pack", 2–5 pt) and Fig 15.16 (the model redesigns, 3–5.5 pt) are unreadable, although exercise 16 and the project depend on reading them. A chapter that teaches "readable text size" should not contain its smallest figures. Three pages lose 25% to a figure pushed onward, and the two-digit exercise numbers are clipped. The three raster images are low-ppi but carry only flat colour cells, so they cause no visible defect.

Page numbers below are **PDF pages**. The printed folio is PDF page − 1 ("n / 39").

Automated checks:
- **Fonts:** all embedded. Chart text is converted to vector outlines, so font sizes were measured from glyph pixel heights at 200 dpi (digit and cap height ÷ 0.73). "₹" falls back to DejaVu Serif (many pages, heaviest on pp. 23, 34, 38), and "→" likewise (pp. 9, 11, 14, 16, 17, 19, 21, 27, 37). No tofu.
- **Images (3 raster, `pdfimages -list`):**
  - p. 18, Fig 15.10: 901×255 px and 901×163 px at **144 ppi**. These are the two heatmap cell grids (matplotlib `imshow`). Cell values, axis labels and titles are vector text on top.
  - p. 37, Fig 15.16 panel E: 317×291 px at **194 ppi**. Also a heatmap cell grid.
  - All three are below the 300 ppi print norm. At 400 dpi the cell edges stay crisp (flat blocks of colour, no interpolation), so nothing blurs in print. Low risk (V15.12).
- **Figure text size (measured):** Fig 15.1 ≈ 7.5 pt; 15.2 5.5–6.4 pt; 15.3–15.5 ≈ 7–7.5 pt; 15.7 ticks ≈ 6.4 pt; 15.11 legend ≈ 5 pt; 15.12 ≈ 7 pt; 15.13 ticks ≈ 5.5 pt; 15.14 ≈ 7 pt; **15.15 2–5 pt**; **15.16 3–5.5 pt**.
- **Text outside margins:** exercise numbers "10."–"25." at x = 48 pt (p. 35). They are clipped (V15.3).
- **Blank-area scan:** p. 6 (67%), p. 21 (68%), p. 31 (72%), p. 36 (77%). p. 40 (33%) is the chapter end.
- Every page was viewed. All 16 figures were zoomed at 200 dpi, and the heatmap images at 400 dpi.

## Patterns (things that repeat on many pages — describe once, list pages)
- **Charts are mostly right-sized.** Matplotlib figures exported at full text width give about 7–7.5 pt, which works. The failures are figures with 3–5 panels in one row, or grids (15.2, 15.11, 15.13, 15.15, 15.16).
- **Figure-driven page breaks** (pp. 6, 21, 31, 36): large figures do not float, so the page before them loses a quarter of its height. Allow figures to float to the next page top while text continues.
- **Colour use** is consistent (Riverstone blue, orange highlight, grey context). The diverging heatmap is red–blue, not red–green. No chart relies on red/green alone. The one red–green case is described in the story (p. 28) and not shown.
- **Code blocks:** SQL has a blue rule. The Python blocks on p. 27 use the grey rule used elsewhere for output, so Python input looks like output (as in Ch14 and Ch27).
- **Tables** (levels of measurement, palettes, distortions, common mistakes) are consistent. "Common mistakes" splits across pp. 28–29 with a repeated header.
- **Folios** "n / 39"; the running footer is correct.
